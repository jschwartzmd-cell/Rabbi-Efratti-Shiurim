Rabbi Ephrati · Morning שיעור on תפילה

The Depth of כוונה in שמונה עשרה

יהיו לרצון, אלוקי נצור, ונפשי כעפר, the Four למען-Phrases, and the Three Steps Back
Recorded ה׳ חשון תשפ״ו · 27 October 2025 · Reworked from the original שיעור
לעילוי נשמת ר' חסקל פלדמן ז״ל, whose יארצייט was on Tuesday;
לעילוי נשמת הרבנית ויינגרטן ליבע שמילצר, on her second יארצייט;
ולעילוי נשמת יאיר בן בנימין ז״ל.

Last week we discussed the פסוק of יהיו לרצון, אלוקי נצור, and the מנהג to bracket אלוקי נצור with יהיו לרצון on both ends. This week we go deeper into the close of שמונה עשרה — the כוונות hidden inside יהיו לרצון, the personal language of אלוקי נצור and ונפשי כעפר, the four למען-phrases that בני קודש say with broken hearts, and the three steps back at the very end.

I. The עשר תיבות, עשר יודי״ן of יהיו לרצון

The ספר סדר היום

Last week we covered the פסוק of יהיו לרצון that we say at the end of שמונה עשרה, and the פסוקים that begin and end with שמותיו של הקדוש ברוך הוא inside אלוקי נצור. We also discussed the מנהג to say יהיו לרצון twice — once before אלוקי נצור and once after.

An important addition belongs here from ספר סדר היום[1]. The סדר היום was written by ר' משה בן מכיר (or בן מוכיר), who lived in צפת in the time of the האריז״ל and the בית יוסף. He is also reported to be the author of a well-known liturgical poem said to date only from the 1500s and to have no earlier source (see note 1).

The סדר היום writes that יהיו לרצון is an extraordinarily significant פסוק. It is a סגולה גדולה to have כוונה in it, and one should say it slowly — not mumble through it. This single פסוק contains everything we asked, every kavvanah we had, every word of שמונה עשרה. In one sentence we are asking הקדוש ברוך הוא to accept everything — even the thoughts we could not express.

A Hidden סוד in the פסוק

The סדר היום points out something fascinating. יהיו לרצון אמרי פי והגיון לבי לפניך ה' צורי וגואלי — the full פסוק in תהלים (י״ט:ט״ו) — contains exactly ten תיבות. It begins with a יו״ד and ends with a יו״ד, and the letter יו״ד appears in it ten times. This is a great סוד. Those who know סודות התורה are מכוון in this פסוק, and if they are מכוון in it, הקדוש ברוך הוא answers their תפילה.

II. The History of אלוקי נצור

It Was Not Originally Part of שמונה עשרה

אלוקי נצור is a relatively recent fixture. If you look at the ראשונים, it is clear that they did not say it. There is one early hint among the גאונים — רב עמרם גאון[2] mentions תפילות like אלוקי נצור — but it was far from universal.

Strictly speaking, אלוקי נצור is not part of שמונה עשרה. Yet over the centuries it became standardized into שמונה עשרה. Today it is so embedded that if you have not yet said יהיו לרצון before it, you cannot answer קדיש, קדושה, or ברכו — you must remain silent, because you are still considered to be in the middle of תפילה.

The אבודרהם[3] — one of the great ראשונים on תפילה — is reported to testify that in his day the Amidah ended with its concluding blessing, after which they took three steps back, and that was the end. אלוקי נצור entered our regular tefillah only later — through the influence of the printed סידור.

Its Source in the גמרא

The full sugya is in ברכות י״ז[4]. There you find a complete page of גמרא listing what each אמורא would add as his personal תפילה after שמונה עשרה. From the dozen or so personal תפילות recorded there (the lecturer said “let's say fifteen”), the printers — beginning with the first modern printed סידור in the 1500s — chose one: the תפילה of מר בריה דרבינא. They placed it at the end of שמונה עשרה, and over time it became so welded to the עמידה that today it carries הלכתי consequences of its own.

Sensitivity in Tefillah The lecturer recalled a beloved rav of the community, whose first יארצייט was then approaching (the recording renders the name as “Rabbi Kinsler”; the spelling is unconfirmed). He was sensitive to the ציבור: he would sometimes skip אלוקי נצור when he saw that the ציבור was waiting for him because he davened a longer שמונה עשרה. "Whoa, the רב didn't say אלוקי נצור!" Relax. It is not the end of the world. We say it, but in such a case you may certainly leave it out — it is not like leaving out a ברכה of שמונה עשרה. It is within the bounds of what חז״ל instituted. The ראשונים we look up to did not say it at all.

III. Why This תפילה of All the אמוראים?

The question is sharp. There were so many beautiful personal תפילות in ברכות י״ז. Why did those who integrated this into our שמונה עשרה specifically choose מר בריה דרבינא's אלוקי נצור לשוני מרע — "guard my tongue from speaking evil"?

We touched on one answer last week. The גמרא (בבא בתרא קס״ה ע״א) teaches that everyone stumbles in לשון הרע, if only in its avak; the lecturer adds that this happens every day. We think we are such צדיקים. Sometimes we actually speak לשון הרע; אבק לשון הרע — the dust of it, the hint of it — is even harder to avoid. Are you really careful about לשון הרע? Out of all the מצוות and עברות in the תורה, this is what we ask הקדוש ברוך הוא specifically to help us with at the close of every שמונה עשרה: please help me guard myself from לשון הרע today.

Another Reading — ושפתי מדבר מרמה

But there is another פשט. Listen to how the אחרונים read this. רבינו, מר בריה דרבינא, was saying, in effect: "I stood before You, הקדוש ברוך הוא, for שמונה עשרה — two minutes, three minutes, ten minutes, whatever — I stood there. And like all of us, I talked. I had no idea where I was sometimes. I mumbled the words. I said and said and said. Sometimes I did not even understand what I was saying."

"רבונו של עולם, please — if I said something and my heart was truly not there, then in some sense I was speaking falsely before You. I was talking to the King and not even present. Forgive me. My lips moved and my mouth said words, but my heart was not in them. Help me to be honest with my mouth and with my heart."

In that light, אלוקי נצור is a kind of apology at the close of שמונה עשרה. הקדוש ברוך הוא, we are honest with You in our vulnerability. We understand that You understand we are human. Please guard us. Help me, in the next שמונה עשרה, to do better — to be more focused, more present, less of a stranger in my own תפילה. A beautiful way to end an עמידה.

IV. ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה

The Plain Reading

We end the תפילה with ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה. עפר — earth, dust, sand. The ראשונים read it like this: I am about to step out of שחרית into my day. I am going to interact with people. And whenever you interact with people there are conflicts, arguments, misunderstandings — that is the human condition. I do not want to fight. I do not want to speak negatively. I do not want to say לשון הרע or שקר. So I tell הקדוש ברוך הוא: those who curse me, those who provoke me — let my soul be like the dust that everyone tramples on. Whatever happens today, I should not have a wrong reaction. You take care of it; I do not need to be involved.

The וילנא גאון — Reward of the Dust

The וילנא גאון[5] writes (the lecturer thought he brings it from the זוהר) something deeper. People want good children. הקדוש ברוך הוא promises the אבות that their children will be like — for אברהם אבינו — ככוכבי השמים, and like עפר הארץ. עפר הארץ. You cannot count the grains of sand any more than you can count the stars.

Says the גר״א: if a yid says, "I want to be like עפר that everyone tramples on" — and despite his ego, despite his urge to fire back — "Are you going to let Yankel humiliate you?! Respond!" — he refuses, and lets himself be like עפר that people walk on, then he merits the reward of dust: that his children will be numerous, like grains of sand, and good. מידה כנגד מידה.

The מהרש״א — Eventually the Dust Wins

The מהרש״א[6] adds a quietly humorous reading. ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה — make yourself like עפר that everyone tramples on. What is the end of the story? Everyone tramples on dust — but what happens at the very end? The dust tramples on us. We all end up beneath the dust. So if you make yourself עפר now, in humility, eventually you will come out on top. People trample on dust today; the dust outlives them. The lecturer adds the prayer that we merit length of days and years.

The Story of ר' חיים מוואלאזשין Two Jews came to ר' חיים מוואלאזשין with a fight over a piece of real estate. "Rebbi, this land is mine!" "No, it's mine!" No one could make peace between them. ר' חיים said, — he addressed one of them as “Reb Yitzchak” — "let me hear what the land says." He took them outside, bent his ear to the קרקע, listened a moment, and stood up. "The קרקע says that you both belong to it. It does not belong to you. You will both end up in the קרקע. What are you fighting for? One hundred and twenty years — and you belong to the קרקע." This is the very lesson of ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה: a person who makes himself like עפר ultimately rises above.

V. Two Schools of Anavah — נאוואַרדאָק and סלאַבאָדקא

This concept was taken to an extreme in נאוואַרדאָק. To put it in context: a few weeks ago we discussed ר' ישראל סלנטר, the founder of the מוסר movement. נאוואַרדאָק represented one wing of that movement, led by the Alter — ר' יוסף יוזל הורוויץ — and was very radical. They emphasized putting oneself down.

The other wing was סלאַבאָדקא, which emphasized גדלות האדם — the greatness of man. You are the crown of creation; you should walk with confidence and pride. (This is, by the way, where certain behaviors in the ישיבה world have their source. You may have noticed older בחורים sometimes treating younger בחורים with a certain air — "I am older, I am holding up the world with my תורה." It comes from somewhere. The thinking was that at sixteen, seventeen, eighteen בחורים should feel they are sustaining the world with their learning. It can go both ways. It can also degenerate into what one might call "Litvish גאוה" — I apologize for the phrase, but it is real. The intention was good; the execution requires discipline.)

נאוואַרדאָק went the other extreme. The בחורים used to walk into a pharmacy and ask for a package of nails — knowing perfectly well that pharmacies do not sell nails — specifically to be shouted at, looked at as nothing, treated as foolish. They did this deliberately as a technique to bring themselves to שפלות, true humility. עפר is the paradigm of this avodah.

VI. The Two Pockets — Self-Esteem and Anavah

Our generation is obsessed with self-esteem. Every day I hear: "self-esteem, self-esteem!" Vadai true — our generation is in a matzav where we need elevation. But come on — we are not children. Why shouldn't we also work on humility?

The רבי ר' בונים מפשיסחא[7] said it best. Every yid must have two pockets. In one pocket the words בשבילי נברא העולם — the whole world was created just for me, just for my avodah, just for my תורה, just for my תפילה. אם אין אני לי, מי לי. In the other pocket: ואנכי עפר ואפר — I am but dust and ash. It is a balance. You need an equilibrium, and you have to be a wise person to maintain it. Every person has his own avodah here.

The famous כלי יקר on פרשת ויצא[8] speaks about exactly this. הקדוש ברוך הוא gives the אבות two analogies for their children: כעפר הארץ and ככוכבי השמים. עפר is humble, simple, a grain of sand. A כוכב is an entire universe. From here it looks small; up close it is enormous. A yid has to know simultaneously: I am a star in heaven, my light shines very far — and I am also a grain of sand. Hold both at once.

VII. The Continuation — "Take Care of It Yourself"

The end of אלוקי נצור speaks about others. Whoever is planning to take me down today, whoever is planning to take advantage of me — a business partner, a friend, somebody saying negative things about me — You, הקדוש ברוך הוא, take care of it. Some נוסחאות add an additional passage right before the closing יהי רצון: that nobody should be jealous of me, that I should not be jealous of them, that I should not get angry today, that they should not get angry at me. We add it.

Personal Kavvanah — Where Do You Connect? Let me share a question I love to ask people: which words in שמונה עשרה do you have the most כוונה in? Where do you find yourself really there, talking to הקדוש ברוך הוא with full attention? Each yid has his own. הלוואי we should all have כוונה through the entire שמונה עשרה.

My own — for whatever it is worth: ותחזינה עינינו בשובך לציון. When we ask הקדוש ברוך הוא to bring us back together, to redeem His scattered sheep, I lose myself. Two thousand years we have been asking for גאולה, for the בית המקדש, three times a day. We beg, we cry, we want the צרות to be over. There is a Yiddish saying: ביז מ'קומט צום קרעטשמע, דארף מען אויך אַ טרינק — until you reach the inn, you also need a drink along the way. They used to travel from city to city by horse and buggy, with inns at intervals. Until the גאולה comes, what will be in the meantime? Make Your children happy. A father wants his children to be happy. If they are not happy, he is not happy. We ask הקדוש ברוך הוא to make us happy until we get to the next inn.

VIII. The Four Phrases — למען שמך

If you add according to נוסח ספרד the closing four phrases, this is one of the most powerful pieces of the entire close of שמונה עשרה. The ראשונים say רבי עקיבא used to say this תפילה, and perhaps רבי אליעזר said parts of it as well (the lecturer's report; not located in the passage of the בית יוסף cited next).

The בית יוסף[9] brings in the name of an aggadic source (הגדה): whoever says these four phrases every day after שמונה עשרה —

עשה למען שמך, עשה למען ימינך, עשה למען תורתך, עשה למען קדושתך.

— is זוכה to be מקבל פני השכינה. The תורה does not often promise that for words said inside שמונה עשרה. These four carry a special weight.

The כוונה

What should one have in mind? The thought is: not for me. I do not deserve all the בקשות I just made. I do not deserve to have them fulfilled. I know my עברות, my thoughts, my failures. So I ask: do it for Your sake. If I have פרנסה, health, good children — it is so that I will be able to serve You. למען שמך, למען ימינך, למען תורתך, למען קדושתך. If you say those words with כוונה, the power is tremendous.

IX. The חתם סופר — קשתי נתתי בענן

The תלמידים of the חתם סופר[10] testified that he said the following vort every single year on פרשת נח. The פסוק says about the rainbow after the מבול: קשתי נתתי בענן[11] — "My bow have I placed in the cloud."

Said the חתם סופר: קשת״י is an acronym for our four phrases:

קדושתך · שמך · תורתך · ימינך.

Breaking Through the Cloud

The גמרא in ברכות ל״ב[12] says — these are difficult words for a yid to hear — מיום שחרב בית המקדש ננעלו שערי תפילה. From the day the בית המקדש was destroyed, the gates of תפילה are closed; no תפילה ascends. But the same גמרא adds: שערי דמעה לא ננעלו — the gates of tears were not closed. You can still beg with tears.

And how do we know that prayer is blocked? The גמרא itself cites a פסוק in איכה (3:8, גם כי אזעק ואשוע שתם תפילתי); the פסוק on which the חתם סופר's vort turns is another, in איכה[13]: סכותה בענן לך מעבור תפילה — He has placed a cloud, and that cloud blocks תפילה from rising.

The חתם סופר said: there is one way to breach that cloud. That is exactly what הקדוש ברוך הוא tells נח. קשתי נתתי בענן — "I have placed קשת״י in that cloud." If you daven not for yourself but say "למען שמך, למען ימינך, למען תורתך, למען קדושתך" — that goes through the cloud. If הקדוש ברוך הוא is blocking our תפילה because of our own merits, but we tell Him "this is for Your sake, for Your Name, for Your תורה, for Your holiness" — He opens it up.

The תלמידים of the חתם סופר said that a yid who has that כוונה in these words, with a broken heart, is guaranteed that his תפילה will be accepted. This is the meaning of the close of שמונה עשרה: I ended my תפילה — and what I said is for Your sake, not for mine. Then קשת״י breaks through the ענן.

X. The Three Steps Back

Source One — The כהן גדול and the Honor of a King

The גמרא in יומא דף נ״ג[14] says that when the כהן גדול left the קדש הקדשים on יום כיפור he did not turn his back — he retreated, walking backward with his face toward the ארון, and the גמרא derives this from שלמה המלך's departure from גבעון. The גמרא extends this: כהנים, לויים, even ישראלים in בית המקדש attending the מעמדות (the rotations of those who came for a week to be present at the קרבנות, discussed in מסכת תענית[15]) did not turn their backs when leaving the מקדש; they turned sideways. The same holds for a תלמיד taking leave of his רבי; the lecturer likened it to leaving a king.

This is true even of leaving one's רבי. People do not do this much today, but I remember when I first went to my ישיבה, ישיבת בריסק, to take the farher with רבי משולם הלוי וייטשיק זצ״ל. He was about to become my רבי for the next three years. I already treated him as a רבי even before he had taught me. When I left after the farher — he had not even told me yet whether I was accepted; it is very hard to get accepted there — I walked out of the room. My father had told me beforehand: when you leave a רבי, when you leave a חסיד'יש Rebbe, you take three steps back. I do not know whether that is why I was accepted, but I did get a call the next day that I had been.

The Stories on דף נ״ג ביומא

The גמרא there brings remarkable examples of תלמידים' respect for their רבנים. רבי אלעזר learned with רבי יוחנן. When רבי אלעזר would leave the שיעור, he walked backwards. If רבי יוחנן left first, רבי אלעזר would stand bent over with his head bowed and wait until רבי יוחנן was out of sight.

The גמרא tells further about רבא, the תלמיד of רב יוסף. רב יוסף was blind. When רבא would leave רב יוסף's presence he would take three steps back — but unlike the standard practice, he would not look behind him. He was so absorbed in humility that he simply stepped back, fell, stumbled, fell again. The גמרא writes that his feet became bloodied from the falls, and the threshold of רב יוסף's house filled with blood. The מפרשים say it was never washed away; the dried blood blackened the threshold.

When they finally told רב יוסף — who was blind and could not see it — that the floor was full of blood, רב יוסף stood up, placed his hands on רבא's head, and said:

יהא רעוא דתרום רישך אכולה כרכא.

“May it be [His] will that you raise your head over the whole city” — the גמרא's words; the lecturer understands the city as מחוזא, where רבא later headed the ישיבה. This is our history. This is how רבא became the רבי of כלל ישראל. The תלמוד הבבלי is full of his words; on every page רבא speaks. He is one of the fathers of the בבלי. Anytime אביי and רבא disagree — and there are roughly three hundred disputes between them — the הלכה is always like רבא, except for the famous six cases of יע״ל קג״ם[16]. All of this — say the מפרשים — is bound up with the כבוד he was so careful to give his רבי: he never turned his back, only walked backward, even at the cost of falling and bleeding.

Source Two — Nevuchadnezzar's Three Steps

The גמרא (סנהדרין צ״ו ע״א)[17] says something striking. Before נבוכדנצר achieved greatness, before he became king, he was only a scribe to the king of Bavel — the גמרא names בלאדן (the lecturer, unsure of the name, said “Evil-Merodach, or whoever it was”). They wrote a peace letter to חזקיהו, מלך יהודה: "שלום to the king, שלום to עם ישראל, and שלום to the great God of the Jews."

When נבוכדנצר returned and saw the letter, he said: "Are you crazy? You wrote G-d last? הקדוש ברוך הוא? You must give כבוד! Put שלום to G-d first, then to the people, then to the king of flesh and blood." They told him: fine, you write it. He rewrote the letter and ran after the messenger to deliver it himself — according to the גמרא, for four steps, until גבריאל stopped him. The lecturer told the story as three steps backward in honor of the שכינה after he had recopied השם at the head of the page.

This is נבוכדנצר — the destroyer of the בית המקדש, the head of the wicked, whom every מדרש names with the suffix ש״ט: שחיק טמיא — "may his bones be ground as dust, like his place." How terrible, how evil, how wicked he was. We still suffer today from what he did to us. But — this one thing he got right. He gave כבוד to הקדוש ברוך הוא in that letter and took three steps back.

And the גמרא says that Heaven rewarded him for those steps (the גמרא says four; the lecturer suggested that the ראשונים had a reading of three). The lecturer adds — these are difficult words to digest — that in their merit הקדוש ברוך הוא granted him the privilege of being the one to destroy the בית המקדש, a link the passage itself does not make explicit. The reverse holds for us: when a yid takes the same three steps back at the close of his עמידה, but for קדושה, we ask עושה שלום במרומיו, הוא יעשה שלום עלינו — and may the בית המקדש be rebuilt speedily in our days. He took three steps back and destroyed; we take three steps back and ask Him to build.

The גמרא in יומא נ״ג ע״א that we cited earlier does not mention the number three for the כהן גדול or the תלמידים; it says that they walked backward and did not turn their backs on their רבי. The number three itself appears on the next page (נ״ג ע״ב): one who finishes praying takes three steps back and says שלום, like one leaving the presence of a king. The lecturer, reporting a source he attributes to a Rabbi Kalish, traced the number to the נבוכדנצר story. We do it — but we do it for the side of קדושה.

XI. רבינו יצחק אבוהב — The Har Sinai Connection

Who He Was

The בית יוסף brings something interesting from one of the great figures in the transition between ראשונים and אחרונים — רבינו יצחק אבוהב, the מהרי״א אבוהב[18]. He was a רב and ראש ישיבה in Toledo, Spain, at the time of the גירוש ספרד of 1492. He was a politically connected leader of כלל ישראל at that time. Together with אברבנאל — דון יצחק אברבנאל, treasurer of Spain and a wealthy man who, it is told, tried to bribe Ferdinand and Isabella ימ״ש to rescind the decree — there was much diplomacy. They tried; ultimately the gates closed.

רבינו יצחק אבוהב fled to Portugal and met with King João II of Portugal, hoping to win the right of return for the Jews. He never succeeded. He died in Portugal in 1493, the year after the גירוש. To this day there is a בית הכנסת in צפת called בית הכנסת אבוהב — part of the regular tour route. Why is it named after a man who died in 1493? Because the legend goes that the ספר תורה in the ארון הקודש of that shul, still standing in 2025, is the ספר תורה that רבינו יצחק אבוהב himself wrote — he was a סופר. A ספר תורה of five hundred or six hundred years. (I do not believe they take it out anymore; I believe it is kept in a safe.) Every time the בית יוסף brings him he calls him רבינו הגדול. He was apparently the גדול הדור. Many things we do today through the בית יוסף come from him.

His Reason for the Three Steps

רבינו יצחק אבוהב says — and this is what I find difficult to understand — the reason we take three steps back: the גמרא in מסכת שבת פ״ח[19] says that when כלל ישראל stood at הר סיני and heard אנכי from the mouth of הקדוש ברוך הוא — they could not bear it. Our physical body could not contain the divine voice. We all fainted. The גמרא says we all actually died. Our נשמות flew back.

Says the בית יוסף in the name of המהרי״א אבוהב: our נשמות flew back three מילין. To remind ourselves that we are standing before the שכינה — and that we really should retreat before such an awesome Presence — we should ideally retreat three מיל. Three מיל would mean walking all the way to the next town; we have just finished מנחה. So we commemorate it with three steps.

A Difficulty I open the גמרא in שבת פ״ח and it does not say three מיל — it says twelve מיל. רש״י brings it on שמות כ׳:ט״ו, the מכילתא (so the lecturer reports) brings it, the גמרא says it — no one says three מיל. I do not understand for the life of me where three comes from. Possibly it is three פרסאות (each פרסה is four מיל, which equals twelve מיל). I really do not know — but this is what the בית יוסף quotes from המהרי״א אבוהב, and it is the reason given for the three steps back at the end of every עמידה.

XII. כבוד שכינה and the ארון קודש

Some have the practice of leaving a shul facing the ארון קודש. This is not a גמרא; the lecturer traced it to the מגן אברהם[20], which derives it from the principle of the גמרא. Just as one does not turn his back on his רבי, so too one does not turn his back on the ארון קודש (the recording says “the שלחן ערוך”). The חסידים who went to the Lubavitcher רבי certainly did not turn their backs on him as they left.

On the basis of the same גמרא, the lecturer says, the שלחן ערוך writes that when we leave the presence of the מלך מלכי המלכים we do not turn our backs (the instruction to look to the sides was not located in שלחן ערוך או״ח קכ״ג). We look to the sides — first of all because someone may be standing within ד׳ אמות still in שמונה עשרה, and it is forbidden to stand there. (Even with our benches between us today, many פוסקים say one should still not do so.) Then we take three steps back.

סיכום (editorial coda — not part of the שיעור)

May we be זוכה to daven and to be answered. May we have כוונה in יהיו לרצון as the סדר היום teaches — slowly, with awareness of the עשר תיבות and the עשר יודי״ן. May our אלוקי נצור be a true apology and a true request. May we hold both pockets — בשבילי נברא העולם and ואנכי עפר ואפר — without losing our balance. May our four למען-phrases break through the ענן with the כח of קשת״י. And may our three steps back, in the שבר־לב of a yid leaving the presence of the מלך מלכי המלכים, bring the building of the בית המקדש במהרה בימינו.

מראה מקומות  ·  Mareh Mekomos

  1. ספר סדר היום, ר' משה בן מכיר (צפת, late 16th century). — ר' משה בן מכיר (also spelled בן מוכיר) was a Safed scholar in the era of the אריז"ל and the בית יוסף. סדר היום is a structured exposition of daily, weekly and yearly עבודת ה' (reportedly first printed Venice 1599; not verified). His remark on the ten תיבות and ten יו"דין in יהיו לרצון אמרי פי (תהלים י"ט:ט"ו) has not been located in the text; it is given as the lecturer reported it. The count itself holds for the full verse (יהיו לרצון אמרי פי והגיון לבי לפניך ה' צורי וגואלי): ten words (counting the two hyphenated pairs as four), beginning and ending with a יו"ד, and ten יו"דין when the Divine Name is counted as spelled out (nine if it is written ה'); this was checked against the Masoretic text. The truncated seven-word quotation printed in the body was corrected accordingly. The lecturer also credited him with the authorship of a famous liturgical poem said to be of the 1500s and to have no earlier source; the recording is unclear, and the AI summary's identification of the poem as יגדל אלוקים חי is not accepted here, since Yigdal is conventionally attributed to an earlier author (R. Daniel ben Yehudah, c. 1300; not checked here). The earlier note's placing of him in עין זיתים and its link to the ten ספירות are not in the source and have been removed. ↩
  2. סדר רב עמרם גאון (9th century). — רב עמרם בן ששנא, גאון סורא (d. ca. 875), composed the earliest extant ordered סדור. The exact form of אלוקי נצור is not standard in his סדור; the פסקי גאונים reference closing personal תפילות at the end of שמונה עשרה as רשות for יחידים. Verify exact placement against ed. Goldschmidt, Jerusalem 1971. ↩
  3. אבודרהם, on סיום שמונה עשרה. — ר' דוד אבודרהם (Seville, mid-14th century). The lecturer reports that in the author's time the Amidah ended with its concluding blessing and three steps back, and that אלוקי נצור was not yet part of it. The passage has not been located, so the wording of the concluding blessing is not asserted (the AI summary gave ברוך אתה ה' עושה השלום; the recording is closer to המברך את עמו ישראל בשלום). The earlier note's statement that אלוקי נצור was "reserved for יחידים" in his time, and the dating of universal incorporation to the early printers (the lecturer says the 1500s), are unverified. ↩
  4. ברכות ט"ז ע"ב – י"ז ע"א — personal תפילות of the sages after שמונה עשרה. — The גמרא records, in succession, the personal closing תפילות of about a dozen sages: on 16b רבי אלעזר, רבי יוחנן, רבי זירא, רבי חייא, רב, רבי and רב ספרא; on 17a רבי אלכסנדרי (the same prayer being ascribed by some to רב המנונא), רבא, מר בריה דרבינא and רב ששת. מר בריה דרבינא's prayer opens אלוקי נצור לשוני מרע ושפתי מדבר מרמה (the last word is מרמה, "deceit"); the phrase ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה occurs partway through it, and it ends ה' צורי וגואלי. The page does not say why this prayer became the standard one; the lecturer's account (the early printers placed it at the end of every שמונה עשרה) is his report and was not located. ↩
  5. ביאור הגר"א; זוהר on ונפשי כעפר לכל תהיה. — The lecturer reports that the גר"א, apparently drawing on the זוהר (he said, "I think he brings it from the…"), reads כעפר in light of the promise to the Patriarchs that their descendants would be like the dust of the earth (בראשית י"ג:ט"ז, to אברהם; כ"ח:י"ד, to יעקב) and the stars (cf. כ"ב:י"ז): one who makes himself like dust merits children as numerous as the dust, מידה כנגד מידה. The location in the ביאור הגר"א and the זוהר passage have not been located. ↩
  6. חידושי אגדות מהרש"א, ברכות י"ז ע"א. — ר' שמואל אליעזר אידלס (1555–1631). The חידושי אגדות on ברכות י"ז ע"א comments on the closing lines of מר בריה דרבינא's prayer, including ונפשי כעפר לכל, referring to תוספות and offering a reading that the phrase continues the preceding petition: his opponents should regard him as dust and not curse him. The lecturer's reading (the dust outlasts the one who tramples it) was not located in the comment as retrieved; it is given as reported. ↩
  7. ר' שמחה בונים מפשיסחא (1765–1827) — the "two pockets" teaching. — ר' שמחה בונים מפשיסחא (1765–1827), a leader of the פשיסחא school of חסידות and the teacher of ר' מנחם מנדל מקוצק (an earlier version of this note called him the founder of פשיסחא and the רבי of קוצק; corrected). His celebrated משל: every yid must carry two slips of paper, one in each pocket. In one: בשבילי נברא העולם (סנהדרין ל"ז ע"א: the משנה says each person must say "the world was created for me"; checked). In the other: ואנכי עפר ואפר (בראשית י"ח:כ"ז). The attribution to him, and the report of it in סיפורי חסידים, are traditional and have not been checked. ↩
  8. כלי יקר on פרשת ויצא, בראשית כ"ח:י"ד. — ר' שלמה אפרים מלונטשיץ (1550–1619). The כלי יקר on בראשית כ"ח:י"ד (והיה זרעך כעפר הארץ) reads the promise in light of אברהם's humility (ואנכי עפר ואפר, י"ח:כ"ז; תהלים ל"ז:י"א וענוים יירשו ארץ) and of the sudden rise from the lowest humility to the highest rank. The pairing of dust and stars as the dual self-awareness of the individual Jew, which the lecturer presents as the כלי יקר's teaching (he said "I think in פרשת ויצא"), was not found in that comment as retrieved; the locus remains unconfirmed. ↩
  9. בית יוסף, אורח חיים סימן קכ"ב, citing an aggadic source. — In או"ח קכ"ב the בית יוסף, discussing אלוקי נצור and the additions said after the תפילה, cites a הגדה (an aggadic source) that one who says "these four things" is זוכה לקבל פני שכינה, in connection with additions that include עשה למען שמך, עשה למען ימינך. The passage as retrieved does not itself list the four phrases or name רבי עקיבא or רבי אליעזר. The lecturer's identification of the "four things" with the four למען-phrases, and his report that ראשונים ascribe the recitation to רבי עקיבא, are given as reported. The first phrase echoes ירמיהו י"ד:ז' (עשה למען שמך; checked); the biblical sources of the other three, listed in an earlier version of this note, were not checked and have been removed. ↩
  10. חתם סופר על התורה, פרשת נח; תורת משה. — ר' משה סופר (Pressburg, 1762–1839). The lecturer reports, on the testimony of the חתם סופר's students, that he repeated annually on פרשת נח the teaching that קשת"י is an acronym of קדושתך, שמך, תורתך, ימינך, which pierces the cloud of סכותה בענן לך מעבור תפילה (איכה ג':מ"ד). The passage was not found in the text of חתם סופר על התורה, נח consulted (the acronym and the word ענן do not appear in the portion retrieved); the teaching is given as reported. ↩
  11. בראשית ט':י"ג — את קשתי נתתי בענן. — הקב"ה's sign of the covenant with נח: the rainbow placed in the cloud (בראשית ט':י"ג). חגיגה ט"ז ע"א: רבי אבא teaches that one who gazes at the rainbow has no regard for the honor of his Maker, citing יחזקאל א':כ"ח (checked). The reading of קשת"י as the initials of the four למען-phrases is the חתם סופר's as reported (see note 10). ↩
  12. ברכות ל"ב ע"ב — ננעלו שערי תפילה; שערי דמעה לא ננעלו. — מיום שחרב בית המקדש ננעלו שערי תפילה ... ואף על פי ששערי תפילה ננעלו, שערי דמעה לא ננעלו (note the singular דמעה). The proof for the first statement is גם כי אזעק ואשוע שתם תפילתי (איכה ג':ח'); for the second, שמעה תפלתי ה' ... אל דמעתי אל תחרש (תהלים ל"ט:י"ג). Checked against the text of ברכות ל"ב ע"ב. One of the most cited חז"ל on תפילה after the חורבן. ↩
  13. איכה ג':מ"ד — סכותה בענן לך מעבור תפלה. — "You have screened Yourself with a cloud, that no prayer may pass through" (checked). The גמרא in ברכות ל"ב ע"ב proves that the gates of תפילה are locked from איכה ג':ח', not from this verse; the cloud verse is the one used in the חתם סופר's teaching as reported. The earlier note's statement that this verse is the רש"י-cited proof-text for the גמרא is not supported and has been removed. ↩
  14. יומא נ"ג ע"ב. — Mishnah (יומא נ"ג ע"א): the כהן גדול leaves the קודש הקודשים by the way he entered, not turning his back; the גמרא derives this from שלמה המלך (דברי הימים ב' א':י"ג). כהנים, לויים and ישראלים at their מעמדות left sideways; likewise a תלמיד leaving his רבי; then רבי אלעזר and רבי יוחנן, and רבא, who walked backward from רב יוסף until his legs were bruised and the threshold of רב יוסף's house was bloodied (all checked). The גמרא spells the name רבא, not רבה. On the next page (נ"ג ע"ב) רב יוסף, told what רבא does, says יהא רעוא דתרום רישך אכולה כרכא; and the three steps backward at the end of prayer are stated (רבי יהושע בן לוי in the name of רבי אלכסנדרי; a ברייתא; אביי and רבא who took the three steps in one bow). The lecturer's remarks that the blood blackened the threshold "according to the מפרשים" and that מחוזא is the city intended were not located. ↩
  15. משנה תענית ד':ב'; תענית כ"ז ע"א — מעמדות. — The משנה (and the גמרא on 27a) describes the משמרות of כהנים and the corresponding groups of ישראלים who stood by the communal offerings on behalf of the people (checked). The גמרא in יומא נ"ג ע"א mentions ישראלים at their מעמדות leaving the Temple sideways. ↩
  16. יע"ל קג"ם — the six exceptions to הלכה כרבא. — The standard mnemonic: in the disputes of אביי and רבא the הלכה follows רבא except in six cases summarized by יע"ל קג"ם. The enumeration of the six and the דף references printed in an earlier version of this note are unverified and have been removed, as has the confused remark about רבה. The lecturer's figure of "two hundred, three hundred" disputes is his estimate. ↩
  17. סנהדרין צ"ו ע"א — נבוכדנצר and the steps for כבוד שמים. — The גמרא (checked in outline): the king בלאדן sent letters to חזקיהו; נבוכדנצר, his scribe, was absent when the letter was written; on his return he objected to the order of the salutations (the "great God" last), had it reversed, and ran after the messenger; גבריאל came and stopped him; רבי יוחנן says that otherwise there would have been no remedy for the enemies of Israel. The passage speaks of four steps (ארבע פסיעות) and of a reward for them, with an a fortiori argument concerning the Patriarchs. The quotation printed in the earlier version of this note ("you ran three steps…, I will give you three kings") is not in the passage and has been removed. The lecturer noted that the גמרא says four and suggested a reading of three; his link between the story and the destruction of the בית המקדש is not stated in the passage as retrieved; the connection to the three steps at the end of prayer he attributes to a Rabbi Kalish (identity unconfirmed). ↩
  18. בית יוסף, אורח חיים סימן קכ"ג, citing the מהרי"א אבוהב. — ר' יצחק אבוהב (traditionally dated 1433–1493), a leading Spanish rabbi at the time of the גירוש ספרד (1492). The lecturer reports that he was ראש ישיבה in Toledo, that he went to Portugal and met King João II seeking permission for the Jews to return, and that he died there in 1493; these details are the lecturer's and were not checked. The בית יוסף in או"ח קכ"ג repeatedly cites רבינו הגדול מהרי"א ז"ל (checked); among the reasons given there for the three steps is that they correspond to the three מילין that ישראל retreated from הר סיני at the giving of the תורה (כנגד ג' מילין שרחקו ישראל מהר סיני בשעת מתן תורה), cited by מהרי"א in the name of a work abbreviated א"ח (presumably ארחות חיים); that מהרי"א is ר' יצחק אבוהב is the lecturer's identification and was not confirmed in the passage. The בית הכנסת אבוהב in צפת bears his name, and its ספר תורה is by tradition his. The earlier note's statements that the expulsion order was lifted in 1493 and that he "led a delegation" to Portugal are not supported and have been removed. ↩
  19. שבת פ"ח ע"ב — מתן תורה and the retreat of the נשמות. — שבת פ"ח ע"ב (checked): רבי יהושע בן לוי teaches that at each utterance ישראל retreated twelve מיל and the ministering angels brought them back, and that at each utterance their souls departed and were revived with the dew of the resurrection. רש"י on שמות כ':ט"ו gives twelve מיל (checked); the מכילתא parallel is the lecturer's report and was not checked. The quotation printed in the earlier version of this note (חזרה נשמתן בטל) was not found. The three מילין of the מהרי"א remain, as the lecturer says, a difficulty; his suggestion that three פרסאות (four מיל each) make twelve מיל is his own. ↩
  20. מגן אברהם, אורח חיים; שולחן ערוך, או"ח קכ"ג ס"א. — The practice of leaving the בית הכנסת facing the ארון הקודש: the lecturer traces it to the מגן אברהם (ר' אברהם הלוי גומבינר, 1635–1682), not directly to the גמרא; the סימן was not located (the מגן אברהם on או"ח קכ"ג as retrieved does not contain it). שולחן ערוך או"ח קכ"ג ס"א (checked): bow, take three steps backward in a single bow, then turn the head to the left (עושה שלום במרומיו) and to the right (הוא יעשה שלום עלינו), and bow forward like a servant taking leave of his master; the gloss adds the custom of saying יהי רצון שיבנה בית המקדש. For the four אמות: או"ח ק"ב:ד' forbids passing in front of one who is praying within four אמות (checked). The earlier note's statement about not returning to one's place before חזרת הש"ץ was not found in ס"א and has been removed. ↩
