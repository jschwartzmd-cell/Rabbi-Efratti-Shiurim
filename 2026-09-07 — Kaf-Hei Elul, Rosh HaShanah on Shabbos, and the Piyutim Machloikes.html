Kaf-Hei Elul, Rosh HaShanah on Shabbos, and the Piyutim Machloikes

Raid-bites on the מחזור — אבינו מלכנו, hilchos ראש השנה שחל בשבת, crying, sleeping, שופר kavanos, נדרים, and the הַגָאוֹנִים-vs-מנהג battle over the פיוטים
Recorded 7 September 2026 · 25 Elul 5786 · Extra Labor Day שיעור (with next Sunday skipped for ראש השנה)

A dense Labor Day שיעור — the third and last before ראש השנה, because next Sunday will be swallowed by יום טוב. The Rav opens on the meaning of כ"ה אלול (Rebbi Eliezer's date for בראשית, Nechemiah's completion of the חומת ירושלים, the Zohar's placement of the fall of מלך רומי and the beginning of גאולה), then rapid-fires through preparation for the מחזור: the sharp textual distinction in אבינו מלכנו between הרוגים על שם קדשך and טבוחים על יחודך (the Danube ma'aseh); the halachic complexities of ראש השנה שחל בשבת (fasting past חצות, drinking coffee before davening — Minchas Elazar's ruling); crying (Shulchan Aruch, Arizal, Reishis Chochma's tear-on-the-forehead); sleeping (Arizal, Vilna Gaon, Mishna Berura); the שבע-fold recitation of למנצח לבני קרח before שופר and the absolute הפסק prohibition once the ברכות begin; הַתָּרַת נדרים; the priority of core תפילה over פיוטים; and — the shiur's climax — the great Geonic-and-Rishonic battle over the פיוטים, with רב האי גאון, רב סעדיה גאון, and the ר"אש (via the Tur) pushing to strip them out, and the Maharil defending them. Closes with the mystery of אלעזר הקליר.

I. Dedication and the Meaning of כ"ה אלול

The Rav opens with a לזכר נשמת dedication: this שיעור is sponsored by Reb Shmuly Klaus and his wife לעילוי נשמת his father-in-law, רב צבי בן רב לוי יצחק ע"ה. May the נשמה have an עלייה through every ברכה made and every דבר תורה learned in the yeshivah.

Today is כ"ה אלול. According to the שיטה of Rebbi Eliezer,[1] today is the day the world was created — בְּרֵאשִׁית בָּרָא אֱלֹקִים. Rashi on the very first pasuk emphasizes that the entire בריאה was בִּשְׁבִיל יִשְׂרָאֵל — for the sake of כלל ישראל.[2] The day carries cosmic weight on top of its Elul-tefillah weight.

Two additional resonances the Rav notes:

  • The חומת ירושלים. Nechemiah's completion of the wall of ירושלים is associated with this date[3] — a communal-historical anchor for כ"ה אלול.
  • The Zohar and the גאולה. The Zohar at the end of בלק[4] hints that on כ"ה אלול the מלך רומי will fall, and the beginning of the גאולה and the ביאת המשיח will unfold. A day of תקוה embedded in the calendar.

With three days to ראש השנה — and the next Sunday שיעור swallowed by יום טוב — the Rav takes today as an extra chance to lay down raid bites: quick, high-density preparations for the מחזור and the עבודת היום.

II. אָבִינוּ מַלְכֵּנוּ — הָרוּגִים vs טְבוּחִים

Returning to אבינו מלכנו (the origin of which — רבי עקיבא at a תעניתת גשמים — was covered yesterday, and codified in the gematria אבינו מלכנו חטאנו לפניך = רבי עקיבא יסדו[5]): today the Rav sharpens a textual distinction inside אבינו מלכנו that most miss.

Two consecutive lines:

אבינו מלכנו עֲשֵׂה לְמַעַן הֲרוּגִים עַל שֵׁם קָדְשֶׁךָ
אבינו מלכנו עֲשֵׂה לְמַעַן טְבוּחִים עַל יִחוּדֶךָ

Why the doubling? What is the difference between הרוגים על שם קדשך and טבוחים על יחודך?

A Story — The Danube, Shema, and the Two Categories

The Rav shares a devastating framework from the Holocaust. Yidden were lined up on the banks of the Danube; the shots came mid-שמע. Those who were shot before they could complete ה' אֶחָד — killed reciting the שם, mouth open on קדושת השם but the יחוד not yet articulated — those are הרוגים על שם קדשך. Those who did finish "ה' אֶחָד" before the bullet, dying with the יחוד actually pronounced — טבוחים על יחודך.

Two categories of mesirus nefesh, two lines in אבינו מלכנו. The Rav suggests saying these two lines with that image in mind — especially in this generation, when the emotional distance to that mesirus nefesh is still not so great.

III. הלכות ראש השנה שחל בשבת

The Fasting Problem

ראש השנה 5787 falls out on Shabbos — שבת שבת (two days of יום טוב, with the first falling on שבת). This creates a serious halachic issue that most people don't take seriously enough: fasting past חצות on שבת is a חמורה prohibition.[6] On a normal Shabbos this is easy — קידוש and the סעודה happen well before חצות. On ראש השנה, when the davening runs long (piyutim, chazaras hashatz with the entire מוסף sequence of מלכיות/זכרונות/שופרות, כהן ברכת), a Yid can easily still be in shul at חצות with nothing in his stomach.

Drinking Before Davening

The prima facie halacha: no eating or drinking before שחרית.[7] The אחרונים discuss:

  • Does water break the ban, or is water permitted (a common lenient position)?
  • Some hold that any שיעור — even a small amount — is forbidden.
  • Some require a שיעור of bread to establish a "meal" before davening.

Practical psak, per the Rav: on ראש השנה שחל בשבת, when services will demonstrably run past חצות, drink coffee before davening.[8] The דאורייתא concern of fasting on Shabbos past חצות trumps the (arguably not דאורייתא) hesitation about drinking before שחרית.

Anecdote — the Minchas Elazar

A Yid known for scrupulous practice came to the מנחת אלעזר (Rav Chaim Elazar Spira of Munkács, 1868–1937) with a chumrah of never drinking before davening — even on Shabbos ראש השנה. The Minchas Elazar's response: drink the coffee. The חשש of fasting past חצות on שבת is real; the chumrah of not drinking before davening is not worth incurring an actual issur חמור to preserve.

IV. Crying on ראש השנה שחל בשבת

שולחן ערוך[9] rules that crying on Shabbos is generally forbidden — it is oneg-negating. The exception: if one derives הנאה (spiritual pleasure) from the tears themselves, the crying is permitted.

On ראש השנה, this is highly relevant. The אריז"ל's view: crying is not the avodas hayom — the day is one of אימת הדין paired with בטחון, not weeping. But tears that come uncontrollably — the נשמה sensing the דין — are acceptable.[10]

A practice from ראשית חכמה:[11] take a tear of תשובה from the eye and wipe it across the forehead, symbolically erasing עוונות from the place where the שם shel כה"ג was inscribed. A tear used this way becomes a כלי of כפרה.

When ראש השנה is on Shabbos, the two categories collide. The prudential psak: uncontrolled tears — from the depth of the נשמה sensing the day — are permitted; performative or forced crying is not. When in doubt, consult a local רב.

V. Sleeping on ראש השנה

שולחן ערוך[12] discourages sleeping on ראש השנה morning — one's מזל should not be dormant at the start of the year. But:

  • Arizal / Vilna Gaon: after חצות, sleep is permitted.[13]
  • Mishna Berura:[14] if you need sleep to gather strength for the עבודה — including תפילת מוסף, תקיעת שופר, and the afternoon תפילה — sleep. Idle awake time is halachically indistinguishable from sleep in terms of the mazal argument; better to be rested and daven with kavanah than awake and depleted.

The Rav's practical bottom line: a nap after חצות, if needed, is not a chumrah worth breaking.

VI. שופר Preparations — לַמְנַצֵּחַ לִבְנֵי קֹרַח and the Absolute הֶפְסֵק

Before תקיעת שופר there is a widespread minhag rooted in the אריז"ל to recite לַמְנַצֵּחַ לִבְנֵי קֹרַח מִזְמוֹר (תהילים מ"ז) seven times.[15] The number seven corresponds to the seven mentions of אֱלֹקִים in the קאפיטל — each recitation "cuts" a level of the שטן's accusation.

The Hefsek Rule

Once תקיעת שופר begins — from the ברכות onward — there is an absolute halachic silence. No talking. No continuing שביעי of לַמְנַצֵּחַ if the ba'al tokea has started the ברכות. No הפסק. Hearing שופר is a מצוה דאורייתא; anything that could constitute a הפסק between the ברכה and the fulfillment is prohibited.[16]

A practical corollary: if you have not finished the seventh recitation and the ba'al tokea starts, stop mid-mizmor. The מצות שופר takes absolute priority.

VII. הַתָּרַת נְדָרִים and Debt Awareness

On ערב ראש השנה, every Yid should perform הַתָּרַת נדרים — the annulment ceremony done in front of three, saying the standard נוסח.[17] Two levels of reflection worth pairing with it:

  • Unfulfilled pledges. Any promise made — to a shul, tzedakah, chavrusa, family — that hasn't been kept, carries נדר-level gravity unless it was framed בְּלִי נֶדֶר. Erev ראש השנה is the moment to either fulfill or formally annul.
  • Future guard. After the הַתָּרָה before the beis din, a Yid can add a private declaration at home: from now on, any promise I make should not have נדר-status unless I explicitly say "נדר." This is the מסירת מודעא some poskim recommend.[18]

VIII. Core Tefilah Over Piyutim — הַמֶּלֶךְ הַקָּדוֹשׁ from Your Place

The Rav is direct: do not rush core תפילה to reach piyutim. פסוקי דזמרה, קריאת שמע and its ברכות, and שמונה עשרה — these are the core. On ראש השנה especially, they should be said with heightened, not diminished, deliberation. If chazaras hashatz's piyutim have to wait an extra two minutes because you're finishing your own שמונה עשרה properly, they wait.

Related — a חסידישע custom the Rav flags. Some שליחי ציבור begin הַמֶּלֶךְ הַקָּדוֹשׁ — the first ברכה of the shatz's repetition — from their place, not the amud. The public coronation of Hashem begins with the tzibbur declaring המלך; only then does the שליח ציבור rise to lead. A small but meaningful gesture.[19]

IX. The Piyutim Battle — הַגָאוֹנִים vs the Minhag

Now the shiur's centerpiece: a great Geonic-and-Rishonic war over whether פיוטים should be said at all in the middle of the תפילה.

Historical Origin — Times of גזירות

In the אמצע-רשי-ראשונים era, particularly during periods of persecution in ארץ ישראל (Byzantine, then early Muslim gezeiros), Yidden were forbidden by decree from davening the standard תפילה. Piyutim were composed as substitutes — they embed halachic content of the day (הלכות of ראש השנה, יום כיפור, פסח, שבועות) inside metrical acrostic-signed poems.[20] A Yid could recite the piyut publicly, be understood by his neighbor, and evade the enforcement mechanism that was targeting the standard nusach.

The Geonic Critique

Once the gezeiros lifted and normal תפילה was restored, the great Geonim pushed back hard against continuing the piyutim inside the tefilah:

  • רב האי גאון (939–1038)[21] — held piyutim in the middle of ברכות שמונה עשרה constitute a הפסק and should be removed.
  • רב סעדיה גאון (882–942) — similar position: the מטבע of the ברכה established by חז"ל should not be altered by outside insertions.
  • הרא"ש — as cited by the טור[22] — endorses this critique: piyutim in the middle of ברכות are הפסק.
  • הוילנא גאון — followed this Geonic line. In many nuschaos following the Gra, piyutim like יוצרות are omitted or repositioned outside the ברכות (before or after שמונה עשרה) to avoid the הפסק issue.[23]

The Defense — Shevach, Minhag, and the Maharil

Despite the critique, both נוסח אשכנז and נוסח ספרד retained piyutim, including the great compositions of שלמה אבן גבירול and יהודה הלוי. Justifications:

  • Piyutim of שבח — pure praise of Hashem, not בקשה — arguably don't constitute the kind of הפסק the Geonim condemned. The Geonic critique was strongest against בקשות inserted into the first-three and last-three ברכות (see yesterday's shiur on זָכְרֵנוּ לְחַיִּים) — pure shevach is a different category.
  • המהרי"ל[24] issued a strong ruling against altering established minhag. Even if a piyut is halachically questionable, changing the מנהג of the ציבור is more dangerous than reciting it. The Maharil's operational directive: learn the piyutim, understand what you're saying, correct grammar errors if they've crept in — but do not change the meaning or omit them from the מנהג.

The Mystery of אֶלְעָזָר הַקַּלִּיר

The greatest paytan — the composer of the bulk of the אשכנז מחזור piyutim — is אלעזר הקליר (or הקָלִיר). His identity and era are contested. Some traditions place him in the era of the תַנָּאִים and identify him with a specific known figure (some say רבי אלעזר בר' שמעון, some say רבי אלעזר בר' יוסי הגלילי). Others place him in the 6th-7th century in ארץ ישראל, closer to the גזירות era that birthed the piyut form. Colorful legends surround him — some traditions describe a bee stinging his tongue giving him the koach to compose,[25] some link him to specific sages.

What is clear: his piyutim form the spine of the אשכנז מחזור, and their language — Hebrew stretched to its limits with rare תנ"ך-derived roots, dense acrostics, elaborate signature patterns — is why serious learning of the מחזור is a lifelong project.

Have a wonderful, wonderful ראש השנה. May הקדוש ברוך הוא grant a כתיבה וחתימה טובה to every Yid, to כלל ישראל, and to the entire world.

  16. הפסק between ברכות של שופר and blowing — absolute prohibition. שולחן ערוך אורח חיים תקצ"ב:ג and משנה ברורה ad loc: no talking between the ברכה of לשמוע קול שופר and the תקיעות, and no talking during the תקיעות themselves. The status of talking after the initial תקיעות but before מוסף (מלכיות/זכרונות/שופרות) is a discussion in the אחרונים. ↩
  17. הַתָּרַת נדרים on ערב ראש השנה. Standard מנהג codified in Machzor and Kitzur Shulchan Aruch. Done in front of three (a beis din of laymen suffices), with the standard נוסח printed in every Machzor. Roots in שולחן ערוך יורה דעה רכ"ח on the general laws of הַתָּרַת נדרים. ↩
  18. מסירת מודעא — pre-annulment of future נדרים. The private declaration made at home (in some ashkenaz traditions on ערב ראש השנה, in others as part of כל נדרי on יום כיפור) that no future statement of intention should carry נדר-status unless the word "נדר" is explicitly said. Codified in שולחן ערוך יורה דעה רי"א:א-ב (with Rama noting that this is the function of כל נדרי). ↩
  19. Chassidishe מנהג — begin המלך הקדוש from your place. Oral chassidishe מנהג. The kavanah: the tzibbur coronates הקדוש ברוך הוא as מלך before the שליח ציבור rises to lead; the שליח ציבור is not the one making Him מלך — the tzibbur is, and the שליח merely joins. ↩
    Flag: chassidishe מנהג — no printed source expected; oral mesorah.
  20. Piyutim originated as substitutes during גזירות in ארץ ישראל. The scholarly consensus (Fleischer, Yahalom, others) is that early piyut developed in ארץ ישראל between the 4th-7th centuries, initially as embellishments and — in the Byzantine period particularly — as substitutes for prohibited standard tefilah. The pattern of embedding halachic content of the day inside metrical acrostic verse is characteristic of the Kalir school and its successors. ↩
    Flag: the "gzeiros forced piyutim as substitute" narrative is one traditional understanding; modern academic scholarship (Fleischer) argues piyut was primarily aesthetic-liturgical development, not necessarily persecution-driven. Both frameworks appear in the mesorah.
  21. רב האי גאון — piyutim in the middle of ברכות = הפסק. Rav Hai Gaon's teshuvos on liturgical matters are cited in later ראשונים (Tur, Beis Yosef) as the strongest Geonic pushback against inserted piyutim. Specific teshuvah locations in שערי תשובה or אוצר הגאונים. ↩
    Flag: exact teshuvah number of רב האי on piyutim as הפסק — cited widely but pin the printed reference.
  22. הרא"ש via the טור — endorses the Geonic critique. The Tur cites Rav Hai and the Rosh together in אורח חיים סימן ס"ח on the question of piyutim inside ברכות of קריאת שמע and of שמונה עשרה. See also Beis Yosef ad loc for the extended discussion. ↩
  23. הוילנא גאון — omitted or repositioned פיוטים. The Gra's נוסח (as recorded in מעשה רב and the Aderes) omits many yotzros and repositions others outside the ברכות. Communities following Nusach HaGra maintain this practice. ↩
  24. המהרי"ל — do not alter minhag; learn the piyutim, correct grammar only. ספר מהרי"ל (מנהגים) — the Maharil (Rav Yaakov Moellin, 1360–1427) codified the אשכנז מנהגים and issued strong rulings against altering communal practice, including on liturgical questions. The rule about correcting grammar but not meaning is specifically preserved in his responses to inquiries from various communities. ↩
    Flag: specific siman in ספר מהרי"ל on piyut-grammar-correction not given in shiur; verify.
  25. אלעזר הקליר — identity and era contested. Traditions vary: some early ראשונים identified him with a tannaic figure (רבי אלעזר בר' שמעון or רבי אלעזר בר' יוסי הגלילי); modern scholarship (based on linguistic and historical analysis) places him in 6th-7th century ארץ ישראל. The "bee stinging the tongue" legend and other hagiographic elements are widely transmitted; specific printed sources vary. ↩
    Flag: the biographical legends around הקליר are oral/hagiographic; scholarly identification remains contested. The tannaitic identification appears in Tosafos (חגיגה י"ג) and elsewhere; modern academic consensus differs.
