Entering Shabbos Shacharis

מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה · תפילין as אוֹת · The Seventh Wing · לְדָוִד בְּשַׁנּוֹתוֹ אֶת טַעְמוֹ
Recorded 9 August 2026 · Reworked from the original שיעור · Sponsored by Rav Eli and Mrs. Goldstein לְעִלּוּי נִשְׁמָתוֹ

Two שיעורים on קריאת שמע שעל המטה closed the arc on מעריב של שבת. This week the שיעור turns to שחרית של שבת — a תפילה that both adds and subtracts from the weekday text. Why do we omit מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה — and where did the Provençal communities fight over it? Why can we not put on תפילין on Shabbos, and what does the story of Rav Shimon Sofer and the Austro-Hungarian Emperor have to do with the halachah? Why does everyone in America daven Shabbos morning at nine — is it a real מנהג or a post-war invention? And why do we add exactly these extra מזמורים — the answer, from a זוהר that Tosfos brings, reaches up to the six wings of the מלאכים and calls us the seventh.

I. Closing One Sugya, Opening Another

The past two weeks were spent on קריאת שמע שעל המטה — the הַמַּפִּיל sugya, the הריני מוחל material, the Maharam of Rothenburg's prison-seder. The Rav opened this week's שיעור with a personal note: several talmidim came over after those two שיעורים to say that they had previously been careless with קריאת שמע שעל המטה, and had begun being מקפיד. That, the Rav said, is the entire point of learning — not entertainment for 45 minutes on Sunday morning, but הלכה למעשה.

We now return to the mainline of the series and open שחרית של שבת. Weekday שחרית was covered extensively earlier in the year — the framework of פסוקי דזמרה, the ברכות of קריאת שמע, שמונה עשרה. Shabbos morning differs in specific ways: certain elements are added, others omitted. The שיעור will spend the next several weeks on what changes and why.

II. What is Different About Shabbos Shacharis?

The Additions

The most visible addition is at the front of פסוקי דזמרה. Many נוסחאות insert a substantial number of extra מזמורים and קפּיטלַך תהילים before בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר or immediately after it. נוסח אשכנז adds relatively few; נוסח ספרד/חסידי adds a great many more — anyone who has walked into a חסידישע shul on Shabbos morning has felt the expansion. Why these specific קפּיטלַך? The Rav returns to this question in Section VIII below — the answer is a striking Zohar cited in Tosfos.

The Omissions

The most conspicuous omission — in most נוסחאות — is מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה, the hundredth chapter of תהילים (פרק ק'). This is one of the great מחלוקות of the Rishonim, and the שיעור opens with it.

III. מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה on Shabbos — the Machloikes of Rashi and the Tur

Rashi's Reading — the מזמור as Stand-In for a קרבן

רש״י בסידורו[1] writes that מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה functions as a verbal substitute for a קרבן תודה — the individual thank-offering brought by one who was saved from danger (traveled the sea, crossed the desert, was released from prison, recovered from illness). On Shabbos, the only קרבנות brought in the בית המקדש were קרבנות צִבּוּר — the תמיד and the מוסף. Individual קרבן יחיד, including a תודה, is not brought on Shabbos. Since מזמור לתודה stands in for the קרבן, Rashi holds it should not be said on Shabbos.

The Tur's Objection

The טור, אורח חיים סימן נ״א[2] dismisses the concern. Saying a פרק of תהילים is not itself חילול שבת; there is no meaningful חשש that when משיח comes tomorrow and this Shabbos will find us in the בית המקדש, a Yid will say "well, I said מזמור לתודה this morning, so I ought to bring the קרבן today too" and thereby be מחלל שבת. Are we suspecting כלל ישראל of that? The Tur rules that מזמור לתודה may — and should — be said on Shabbos.

The Structure of the מזמור — Four Pesukim, Four Loaves

The dispute is sharpened by a striking structural detail. מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה is short: after the opening title-verse, it contains exactly four pesukim. The מפרשים[3] note that this is not arbitrary. The קרבן תודה was brought with a specific accompaniment: forty loaves, in four categories — ten of חלות מצות, ten of רקיקין, ten of חלות רבוכה, and ten of חמץ. Four pesukim, four types of loaves. The רמז ties the פרק to the physical structure of the offering. For Rashi, this remez is precisely the point — the מזמור is a textual תודה — and so the omission on Shabbos follows the physical omission of the קרבן. For the Tur, the remez is beautiful literature but does not extend to a שבת prohibition.

The Regional Split — France, Spain, and the Battleground of Provence

The מחלוקת mapped onto a regional divide. אשכנז (particularly צרפת, where Rashi's authority was decisive) followed Rashi — omit on Shabbos. ספרד followed the Tur — recite. And in between the two — in the border region of פּרוֹבַנְס — communities oscillated. Some pulled toward the French tradition, some toward the Spanish, and the Provençal קהילות were the site of vigorous debate. It is one of the small pleasures of learning the Rishonim to see how the same halachic dispute became a geographical fault-line.

A Parallel Omission — ערב פסח and פסח

A supporting point for Rashi. הלבוש[4] observes that מזמור לתודה is also omitted on ערב פסח and throughout פסח. The connection: the קרבן תודה included ten loaves of חמץ, and no one would bring such an offering when חמץ is prohibited or in danger of leaven. That we omit the מזמור on these days as well strengthens the reading that the recitation is closely bound to the physical קרבן. The Levush notes it as reinforcement for the Ashkenaz practice.

Our Local Minhag

The Rav noted that נוסח אשכנז and most Ashkenazi קהילות today follow Rashi and omit; some Spanish/Sefardi communities follow the Tur and recite. Know your נוסח and follow your מנהג המקום.

IV. Why No תפילין on Shabbos — the Sugya of אוֹת

The גמרא and the Three Signs

The גמרא בברכות[5] teaches that on Shabbos, we do not put on תפילין because שבת is itself an אוֹת — a "sign" of our covenant with הקב״ה. Three מצוות are called אוֹת in the Torah: מילה, תפילין, and שבת. Each testifies to the same covenantal fact from a different angle.

The Maharsha in the Name of the סמ״ג — Two Witnesses Suffice

The מהרש״א על ברכות דף י״ד, citing the סמ״ג[6], explains the mechanism beautifully. The Torah's principle in monetary and legal matters is עַל פִּי שְׁנֵי עֵדִים יָקוּם דָּבָר (דברים י״ט:ט״ו) — "on the mouth of two witnesses shall a matter stand." Two witnesses suffice; a third is not required. On any weekday, a Yid has two witnesses to the covenant: מילה on his body and תפילין on his arm and head. On Shabbos, the two witnesses are מילה and שבת itself. תפילין would be a superfluous third witness — and, more than superfluous, inappropriate.

Not Merely Unnecessary — a זִלְזוּל בְקְדוּשַׁת שַׁבָּת

The ספרים הקדושים go further. Putting on תפילין on Shabbos is not merely superfluous; it is a זלזול בקדושת שבת — a diminishment of Shabbos's own sanctity. It implies that שבת's אוֹת is insufficient, that another sign is needed to complete the testimony. On this understanding, תפילין on Shabbos are מוקצה and prohibited outright.

A Mashal from Rav Shimon Sofer and the Kaiser

The Rav told the story of ר' שמעון סופר, chief rabbi of Krakow and son of the חתם סופר. In his day, the קהילה was informed that the Kaiser — the Austro-Hungarian Emperor, Franz Joseph — would visit the שוּל on Shabbos morning. It was a major event: the crown coming to acknowledge the Jewish community.

A reform provocateur, jealous that the Emperor was visiting the חרדי synagogue rather than the reform temple, snuck into the shul the night before, took down the large portrait of Franz Joseph that hung in the lobby (the community had put it up as a customary sign of allegiance), and hid it. He then sent word to the Emperor's court: Watch out — the Jews normally have your portrait, but they have taken it down as a rebellion against the crown.

The Emperor arrived Shabbos morning with his entourage. The first thing he did was look for the portrait. "Where is the picture I heard was hanging here?" The reform provocateur was smiling — the trick was working. Rav Shimon Sofer, on the spot, produced the answer:

"Dear Emperor — there is a law in Jewish practice. The whole week we put on תפילין. On Shabbos, we do not. Why? Because on Shabbos, the King Himself — הקב״ה — is here. During the week, when the King is not visibly present, we need a sign of our connection. When the King is present in person, we need no sign. So too with your portrait: during the week we hang your picture as a reminder of your presence and our allegiance. But when you yourself are here — the portrait would be superfluous, and even a diminishment of the honor. We put the portrait away for the duration of your visit."

The Emperor was נִשְׁפַּל — deeply impressed. A קידוש השם. The משל is admittedly kinderish, the Rav conceded, but it captures the point exactly: שבת is the presence of the King, and תפילין on that day would be a portrait when the King is visibly in the room.

יום טוב and חול המועד

A related question is whether יום טוב shares Shabbos's אות-status, since it is also called שבתון. The אחרונים[7] discuss this, and the Zohar treats יום טוב as similar to Shabbos in this regard. The practical consequence is the machloikes over תפילין on חול המועד — a live dispute with variant מנהגים across קהילות. The שיעור deferred this discussion to when the series reaches תפילות של יום טוב.

V. The Time of Shabbos Shacharis — Nine O'Clock, and Where It Came From

The American Norm

A near-universal feature of American Orthodox life: Shabbos morning shul begins at 9:00 AM — sometimes 8:45, occasionally 8:30, but almost never earlier. The main sanctuary of the local shul, the yeshiva, the shtiebel — all begin close to nine. Even the "eight-thirty" minyan is a self-consciously early option. In ארץ ישראל the practice is different: most מנינים begin by 7:00 AM or 8:15 AM, partly driven by the heat. Is the late American start a real minhag with roots — or a modern American invention?

Rav Belsky's Pet Peeve The Rav shared a personal מעשה. When he lived in Miami, his wife's grandfather, הרב ישראל בעלסקי זצ״ל[16], spent a Shabbos with them. Shabbos morning, the family (whose father-in-law was the ראש כולל of a ליטוויש כולל) walked out the door at 8:00–8:15 AM. The Rav stayed on the couch waiting for his 9:00 AM shul at the Kerestir chassidishe medrash. Rav Belsky turned to him: You're not coming? "I have a nine o'clock." He was not happy. He said the whole institution of Shabbos-morning shul at nine is a post-war innovation and — with a smile — "it's all the chassidim's fault." He grew up in Williamsburg and said that pre-war, in old Williamsburg, other than a few chassidishe rebbelach, no minyan started later than 8:15 or 8:20. His pet peeve: a 9:00 AM start risks missing זמן קריאת שמע and sometimes even זמן תפילה.

The Ramo — a Later מנהג for Shabbos Morning

Nonetheless, the practice does have a source in the Rishonim. The רמ״א, אורח חיים סימן רפ״א:א[9] writes that the מנהג is to daven שחרית later on Shabbos than during the week. The Ramo does not say precisely how much later — "a little later" — but the framework is his.

The מרדכי and the Tradition from רב האי גאון

The מרדכי[10] traces this custom to a striking source. He records a tradition heard in the city of Rome — one of the Rishonim met רב האי גאון (the last of the Geonim) there and heard from him that on Shabbos morning we daven שחרית later. The reason: a subtle textual distinction in the pesukim describing the daily קרבנות.

For the daily תמיד של שחר, the Torah says: "אֶת־הַכֶּבֶשׂ הָאֶחָד תַּעֲשֶׂה בַבֹּקֶר" (במדבר כ״ח:ד) — "the one lamb you shall offer in the morning" — and more emphatically, "בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר", implying an early morning obligation.

For Shabbos, the Torah opens: "וּבְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת" (במדבר כ״ח:ט) — without the "בַּבֹּקֶר." The absence, the tradition reads, implies that the early-morning pressure of the weekday תמיד does not apply on Shabbos.

The Textual Difficulty — The Pasuk Is About מוסף, Not תמיד

The אחרונים immediately raise the difficulty: the pasuk "וּבְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת" introduces the מוסף of Shabbos, not the morning תמיד. It stands to reason that מוסף is later than שחרית — of course the pasuk about מוסף does not say "בַּבֹּקֶר." That the pasuk about מוסף omits "morning" cannot ground a distinction about the morning תמיד.

המהרי״ל[11], building on the Mordechai, sharpens the claim: even the morning תמיד of Shabbos was brought later than the weekday's — the כהנים in the בית המקדש slept later on Shabbos morning. But this reading is contested and the pasuk-based proof remains difficult.

The רדב״ז — a Beautiful Defense, and a Personal Repudiation

The רדב״ז[12], in a famous תשובה, mounts a stirring defense of the מרדכי's tradition. Since the tradition comes from רב האי גאון — the last of the Geonim, one of the great authorities of חז״ל's שלשלת — his words must be treated as דברי קבלה. If we do not fully understand the reasoning, we are obligated to assume that a deep truth is there even if it eludes us.

And then, at the end of the same תשובה, the רדב״ז delivers the twist. He writes: Do not think, dear reader, that because I have defended Rav Hai Gaon at length, I therefore rely on this tradition myself. חַס וְשָׁלוֹם that I should sleep late on Shabbos morning and daven late. He calls the late-davening minhag not a מנהג יפה, not a מנהג הגון, and states plainly that he himself davens ותיקין every Shabbos.

The Gemara in מגילה — Yom Tov is Late, Shabbos Should Be Early

There is a further difficulty. The גמרא מגילה דף כ״ג ע״א[13] teaches that on יום טוב, one davens late. Why? Because Yom Tov allows cooking, and one wakes early to prepare the seudah — flipping steaks, tending the meal — and comes to shul later, relaxed. On Shabbos, when nothing can be cooked and everything is prepared in advance, there is no such distraction; one should come early. רש״י שם comments explicitly: on Shabbos one should daven ותיקין. This reading — that Shabbos actually pushes earlier davening than the weekday, not later — cuts directly against the מרדכי's tradition.

VI. The Simpler Reasons — עונג שבת and Learning Before Davening

Reason 1 — שֵׁינָה בְּשַׁבָּת תַּעֲנוּג

Many אחרונים offer a more straightforward reason: עונג שבת. The rule "שֵׁינָה בְּשַׁבָּת תַּעֲנוּג" — "sleep on Shabbos is a delight" — is itself a category of Shabbos כבוד/עונג. The pasuk-based argument from Rav Hai Gaon is not the true reason; it is an אַסְמַכְתָּא — a textual anchor for a pre-existing rationale.[14] Shabbos is a day of rest; the community allows itself a later start to honor the spirit of the day. The pasuk merely hangs the practice on a textual peg.

Reason 2 — Not Late Sleep, But Early Learning

Another Rishon offers a very different reading of the same מנהג. It is not that people slept late; they woke up at the regular time and used the intervening hours learning, specifically the weekly obligation of שנים מקרא ואחד תרגום[15]. On this reading, the later davening is a gift only to those who fill the time with תורה. It is not permission to sleep in; it is a window for extra limud. If one wakes up at the regular time and does not learn, the later davening loses its היתר.

A Contemporary Caution

Whichever reason one adopts, the halachic guardrail is the same. A 9:00 AM מנין can — in the summer or on a fast-moving day — run up against סוף זמן קריאת שמע or even סוף זמן תפילה. If the שליח ציבור is behind pace, one must be prepared to stop in the middle of פסוקי דזמרה and recite קריאת שמע oneself to fulfill the מצוה דאורייתא. The משנה ברורה writes clearly: at minimum, say the first פסוק of שמע with כוונה; and the safer path is to say the entire קריאת שמע.

VII. The Voice of Tefillah — בְּלַחַשׁ in Weekday, בְקוֹל וּבְנִיגוּן on Shabbos

A distinction most oylam does not know. The מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה, citing the אריז״ל[17], distinguishes between the volume of פסוקי דזמרה during the week and on Shabbos. During the week, פסוקי דזמרה is said בְלַחַשׁ — quietly, under the breath. On Shabbos, it should be said aloud, with נִיגוּן, expansively and joyfully. Walk into a chassidishe shul on Shabbos morning and the difference is audible: the entire פסוקי דזמרה is sung. This is not a חסידישע affectation; it is the אריז״ל's reading, brought להלכה in the Mishnah Berurah.

The Shaliach Tzibbur and the Impatient Oilam

The Rav pressed the point with a warning from the מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה[18]. If the שליח ציבור is being מַאֲרִיךְ — taking his time on פסוקי דזמרה, singing with כוונה — and a member of the tzibbur wants to protest ("nu, kum shoin, let's go, we have to learn afterward"), the משנה ברורה writes that this protest is prohibited even if the person's motive is לשם שמים — even if he genuinely wants to finish davening earlier so he can learn more תורה. The עיקר דין is that פסוקי דזמרה is meant to be said בְנַחַת וּבְכוונה. Shabbos gives extra time; the שליח ציבור is entitled to use it.

VIII. The Zohar's Seventh Wing — Why These Extra מזמורים

The Six Wings of the מלאכים

Now to the deeper question: why do we add exactly these extra קפּיטלַך on Shabbos? The Rav returns to a זוהר brought in תוספות, סנהדרין דף ל״ז[20]. The פסוק in ישעיהו ו׳:ב[21] describes the שרפים: "שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם לְאֶחָד" — "six wings, six wings to each one." The מלאכים have six wings.

One Wing Per Day — But What About the Seventh?

Each of the six wings sings שירה on one day of the week. Sunday one wing, Monday another, and so through the six weekdays. When Shabbos arrives, the מלאכים have no wing left. They come to הקב״ה and ask: "מי ישיר היום?" — who will sing today? הקב״ה answers: כנסת ישראל. You are the seventh wing.

Shabbos is "אוֹת הִיא בֵּינִי וּבֵין בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל לְעֹלָם" (שמות ל״א:י״ז) — the sign between הקב״ה and His children. On the day when the connection is most intimate, הקב״ה does not want the שירה of the מלאכים; He wants ours. A Yid on Shabbos is higher than a מלאך.

The Practical Implication

This is the reason for the added קפּיטלַך in פסוקי דזמרה on Shabbos morning. Our extra מזמורים are the שירה of the מלאכים, now sung by כלל ישראל. When a Yid on Shabbos says "מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת" (תהילים צ״ב) or "יִשְׂמְחוּ צַדִּיקִים בַּה'" (תהילים צ״ז:י״ב) or the other Shabbos additions, he is embodying the seventh wing. Different נוסחאות select different פרקים, but the theme is uniform: בריאת העולם and מתן תורה — the two great acts הקב״ה chose to associate with שבת קודש.

IX. בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and יִשְׁתַּבַּח — The No-Talking Framework

A ברכה מסגרת

בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and יִשְׁתַּבַּח form a בְּרָכָה מַסְגֶּרֶת[22] around פסוקי דזמרה — an opening ברכה and a closing ברכה that bracket the entire unit. Just as one may not speak between a ברכה on food and the eating of the food, one may not speak between בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and יִשְׁתַּבַּח. The שיחת חולין is prohibited; only דבר מצוה or צורך גדול is permitted.

A Practical Difference — נוסח אשכנז vs. נוסח ספרד

The two נוסחאות create a very different practical experience on Shabbos morning.[23] נוסח אשכנז begins בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר early — soon after the opening הודו and the introductory pesukim. From that point through יִשְׁתַּבַּח, no talking. נוסח ספרד adds many additional מזמורים before בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר — creating the mistaken impression that there is a "talking window" for longer. The Rav noted his own history: as a bochur he davened נוסח אשכנז and was jealous of the נוסח ספרד boys who seemed to have extra pre-בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר conversation time. Then he switched. But the correct halachic instinct is the opposite: the shift is only about when the framework opens, not about whether one should ever be schmoozing.

A Sharp Word from the Rav The Rav did not soften this: someone who says בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and then schmoozes with his neighbor through פסוקי דזמרה is being עם הארץ. It is not a chumrah — it is basic halachah, and a friend saying בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר may (and should) be reminded that from this point on, no talking.

X. If You Come Late — What to Prioritize

A Shabbos-morning reality: many people arrive late and need to catch up quickly. The מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה[19] gives a clear order of priorities:

  1. בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר — the opening ברכה of פסוקי דזמרה.
  2. אַשְׁרֵי (תהילים קמ״ה) — the structural centerpiece of פסוקי דזמרה.
  3. יִשְׁתַּבַּח — the closing ברכה.
  4. נִשְׁמַת כָּל חַי — the Shabbos-specific centerpiece of the extended שירה.

The משנה ברורה is explicit: the extra קפּיטלַך we add on Shabbos are not מעכב. If time is tight, they may be omitted and made up later if desired. But נִשְׁמַת כָּל חַי — that is not to be missed. It is the central Shabbos piece of the entire שירה.

XI. לְדָוִד בְּשַׁנּוֹתוֹ אֶת טַעְמוֹ — David's Madness and Its Shabbos Connection

The Story

Many נוסחאות add תהילים ל״ד[24] to the Shabbos morning additions. The פרק's superscription is striking: "לְדָוִד בְּשַׁנּוֹתוֹ אֶת טַעְמוֹ לִפְנֵי אֲבִימֶלֶךְ" — "Of David, when he changed his demeanor before Avimelech." Why does this פרק belong on Shabbos morning? The Rav told the story.

After דָּוִד killed גָּלְיָת, שאול המלך — who had promised his daughter Michal to the slayer — grew consumed with jealousy. דָּוִד was forced to flee. He ran to אָכִישׁ מֶלֶךְ גַּת, capital of the פלשתים (in ספר שמואל א' פרק כ״א). ("Avimelech" is the generic title for kings of Plishtim, as "Paro" is for kings of Egypt.) Arriving at Achish's court, דָּוִד was recognized: "This is the warrior about whom they sing in the streets of ישראל — he killed Golias, our greatest gibor!" He was in mortal danger.

דָּוִד did something he never did before or after in his life: שִׁנָּה אֶת טַעְמוֹ — he acted mad. He drooled, spoke incoherently, played the lunatic. Achish, watching, dismissed him with the famous line: "חֲסֵר מְשֻׁגָּעִים אֲנִי?" — "Am I short of madmen?" — and had him expelled. דָּוִד was saved.

Why It Worked — a Detail from חז״ל

חז״ל[25] note that Achish's own household already had two משוגעים — his wife and his daughter — screaming through the palace all day. When דָּוִד began carrying on in the courtyard, Achish snapped: "I already have a headache from mine — I don't need another!" — and expelled him. That domestic detail tipped the scales that saved דָּוִד's life.

Why This Story on Shabbos?

The Rav closed the שיעור with the connection.[26] The story of דָּוִד's deliverance before אָכִישׁ took place on Shabbos. That is the tie-in. תהילים ל״ד is דָּוִד's שיר של הודאה composed after that deliverance — the great pasuk "אֲבָרְכָה אֶת ה' בְּכָל עֵת; תָּמִיד תְּהִלָּתוֹ בְּפִי" and the following psalm of בטחון and praise. It is a שירת הודאה born of vulnerability — the הודאה of a fugitive who was saved, not the הודאה of a conqueror. That, the Rav suggested, is the exact hashkafic register of Shabbos morning: not the אנגליזיסטע shirah of the מלאכים at their strongest, but the שירה of Klal Yisrael from the position of חסרון and אמונה. This is the שיר הקב״ה chose over the מלאכים's on Shabbos — and this is the שיר we sing.

XII. Practical Takeaways

  • Know your נוסח. Identify which מזמורים your סידור adds on Shabbos and understand where בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר falls.
  • מִזְמוֹר לְתוֹדָה: follow your community's מנהג. The vast majority of Ashkenaz omits (per Rashi); some Sefardim recite (per the Tur).
  • תפילין: not on Shabbos. Not merely superfluous but a זלזול in Shabbos's own אוֹת-status.
  • Timing: even with a 9:00 AM מנין, guard סוף זמן קריאת שמע and סוף זמן תפילה. Stop in the middle of פסוקי דזמרה if needed to say שמע at zman.
  • פסוקי דזמרה: בְנַחַת וּבְכוונה. From בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר through יִשְׁתַּבַּח: no talking. On Shabbos, say it aloud, with נִיגוּן — you are the seventh wing.
  • Late arrival: בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר, אַשְׁרֵי, יִשְׁתַּבַּח, נִשְׁמַת. Extras are not מעכב. נִשְׁמַת is.

מראה מקומות — Extended Sources

  1. רש״י בסידורו — מזמור לתודה omitted on שבת — Rashi's סידור (surviving in manuscript form, published in modern editions from the 19th century onward) records the practice of omitting מזמור לתודה on Shabbos. Rashi's reasoning is that the מזמור functions as a substitute-recitation for the physical קרבן תודה — an individual thank-offering brought by one saved from danger (see משנה זבחים ה:ו for the categories). Since קרבנות יחיד are not brought on Shabbos (only קרבנות ציבור — the תמיד and מוסף — are offered on Shabbos in the בית המקדש), the verbal substitute is also omitted. This ruling was carried into the standard Ashkenaz נוסח via מנהג צרפת. ↩
    Note: The Rav cited "רש״י בסידורו" from oral tradition. The manuscript siddur attributed to Rashi is well-attested but the specific formulation of the omission-reason may vary between manuscripts. Standard reference for the reasoning is via the Tur's summary of it below.
  2. טור, אורח חיים סימן נ״א — challenges Rashi's omission — The Tur, in his codification of the laws of פסוקי דזמרה (סימנים נ״א–נ״ג), records both positions and rules against Rashi's omission. His argument: reciting a פרק of תהילים is not itself חילול שבת, nor is there a real חשש that when the בית המקדש is rebuilt, a Yid will conclude from the מזמור-recitation that he ought to bring the physical קרבן on Shabbos. The Tur therefore holds one should say מזמור לתודה on Shabbos as on any other day. The practice was preserved in מנהג ספרד. ↩
  3. Four pesukim = four types of לחמי תודה — מזמור לתודה (תהילים ק') consists of an introductory verse ("מזמור לתודה, הריעו לה' כל הארץ") followed by four content-verses. The מפרשים note that the קרבן תודה was accompanied by forty loaves in four categories — ten חלות מצות, ten רקיקין, ten חלות רבוכה, and ten חלות חמץ (see משנה מנחות ז:א). The four content-pesukim serve as a רמז to the four categories of loaves, sharpening Rashi's reading that the מזמור functions as a verbal-substitute for the physical קרבן structure. ↩
  4. הלבוש — מזמור לתודה also omitted on ערב פסח and פסח (חמץ) — The לבוש (ר' מרדכי יפה, 1530–1612), in his commentary on Orach Chaim on the laws of Passover and the laws of פסוקי דזמרה, observes that מזמור לתודה is omitted not only on Shabbos but also on ערב פסח and throughout חג הפסח. The reason: the קרבן תודה included ten loaves of חלות חמץ, and the offering itself would be problematic in a season when חמץ is prohibited. The parallel omission reinforces the reading that the מזמור-recitation is tightly bound to the physical structure of the קרבן (per Rashi/fn1), and is not merely a general song of thanksgiving. ↩
  5. גמרא ברכות דף י״ד — Shabbos as אוֹת — The sugya of Shabbos as אוֹת — sign of the covenant — appears in מנחות דף ל״ו ע״ב ("שבתות וימים טובים לאו זמן תפילין נינהו — שכן איקרו אות") and in related passages in Berachos. Three mitzvos are called אוֹת in Torah: מילה (בראשית י״ז:י״א — "והיה לאות ברית ביני וביניכם"), תפילין (שמות י״ג:ט — "והיה לך לאות על ידך"), and שבת (שמות ל״א:י״ז — "ביני ובין בני ישראל אות היא לעולם"). Each testifies to the same covenantal relationship from a different angle. The Rav cited the sugya as being at "ברכות דף י״ד and דפים צ״ו–צ״ח"; the primary source on תפילין/אוֹת is מנחות ל״ו ע״ב. ↩
    The Rav cited ברכות דף י״ד from memory as the primary sugya source; the canonical treatment appears in מנחות ל״ו. The Berachos daf may contain the passing reference in the sugya of ק״ש/תפילין at ברכות דף י״ד–ט״ו.
  6. מהרש״א ברכות י״ד, בשם סמ״ג — עַל פִּי שְׁנֵי עֵדִים יָקוּם דָּבָר — The מהרש״א (ר' שמואל אליעזר איידלס, 1555–1631), in his חידושי אגדות on ברכות דף י״ד, cites the סמ״ג (ר' משה מקוצי, mid-13th c., in his ספר מצוות גדול, מצוות עשה of תפילין) for a beautiful reading of the tefillin-on-Shabbos exclusion. The principle from דברים י״ט:ט״ו — "עַל־פִּי שְׁנֵי עֵדִים אוֹ עַל־פִּי שְׁלֹשָׁה־עֵדִים יָקוּם דָּבָר" — establishes that two witnesses are legally sufficient; a third is not required. Applied to the covenantal signs: each day requires two עֵדִים (witnesses/signs) to the Yid's covenantal relationship with הקב״ה. Weekdays: מילה + תפילין. Shabbos: מילה + שבת. On Shabbos, adding תפילין would be a superfluous third witness. The rest of the Rishonim's reading (זלזול, מוקצה) builds on this framework. ↩
  7. Zohar / אחרונים — Yom Tov and Chol HaMoed as שבתון — The status of יום טוב regarding אוֹת and תפילין is disputed. The Zohar (in פרשת פנחס and elsewhere) treats יום טוב as similar to Shabbos in this regard, since יום טוב is also called שבתון. The אחרונים dispute whether this extends to חול המועד. The שולחן ערוך אורח חיים סימן ל״א records the machloikes; some communities put on תפילין on חול המועד (following the Rambam and Ashkenazi Rishonim), some do not (following the Zohar and the Ari"zal). The הגר״א famously ruled — based on the Zohar — not to put on תפילין on חול המועד; many Litvish yeshivos follow this practice, as do most חסידישע קהילות. Other Ashkenazi communities (following the Rambam and earlier Ashkenazi Rishonim) do put on תפילין on חול המועד without a ברכה. The Rav deferred fuller discussion to when the series reaches תפילות של יום טוב. ↩
  8. רמ״א, אורח חיים רפ״א:א — later davening on Shabbos morning — The שולחן ערוך, אורח חיים סימן רפ״א:א with the הגהת הרמ״א deals with the order and timing of Shabbos morning תפילה. The Ramo's addition states the מנהג of davening שחרית of Shabbos later than during the week. The Ramo does not specify precisely how much later — his phrasing is that it is done "somewhat later." He gives no reason in the siman itself; the reasoning is developed by the מרדכי (fn10) and the אחרונים. ↩
  9. המרדכי בשם רב האי גאון — pasuk-based tradition — The מרדכי (ר' מרדכי בן הלל, late 13th c.), in his commentary on Shabbos, cites a tradition. One of the Rishonim was in Rome and met רב האי גאון (the last of the Geonim, d. 1038), who told him that on Shabbos morning one davens שחרית later. The reason is textual: for the daily תמיד של שחר, the Torah says "בַּבֹּקֶר בַּבֹּקֶר" (במדבר כ״ח:ד and elsewhere), implying an early-morning obligation. For Shabbos, the Torah opens "וּבְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת" (במדבר כ״ח:ט) without "בַּבֹּקֶר," implying no such early-morning pressure. The textual difficulty (that "וּבְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת" introduces מוסף, not תמיד) is addressed by fn11 and the אחרונים. ↩
    The Rav cited this as coming from "the Mordechai in the name of Rav Hai Gaon via a Rishon in Rome." The tradition is well-attested in the Mordechai and cited by the Ramo. Standard reference: Mordechai on Shabbos פרק אחד עשר (הזורק).
  10. מהרי״ל — כהנים slept later on Shabbos morning — The מהרי״ל (ר' יעקב מולין, ~1360–1427, "father of Ashkenaz minhagim"), building on the מרדכי's tradition, sharpens the reading: even the morning תמיד of Shabbos in the בית המקדש was brought later than the weekday's, because the כהנים slept later on Shabbos morning. This attempts to close the textual difficulty in the מרדכי (that "וּבְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת" is about מוסף, not תמיד) by claiming that even the תמיד of Shabbos was pushed later. The reading is contested; see the רדב״ז's discussion in fn12. ↩
    The Rav cited the Maharil from memory. Standard source: מהרי״ל, הלכות תפילה. The specific claim about the Kohanim's timing appears in the Maharil's discussion of Shabbos morning timing.
  11. הרדב״ז — defends Rav Hai Gaon as דברי קבלה, but personally davens ותיקין — The רדב״ז (ר' דוד בן זמרא, 1479–1573, chief rabbi of Egypt), in a famous תשובה, defends the מרדכי's tradition from Rav Hai Gaon. His argument: since רב האי גאון was one of the greatest authorities in the חכמים's שלשלת, his words must be treated as דברי קבלה — received tradition whose deep truth must be trusted even if we do not fully understand it. But at the close of the תשובה, the Radvaz delivers the punchline: do not think I therefore rely on this myself — חַס וְשָׁלוֹם that I should sleep late on Shabbos morning and daven late. He calls the late-davening minhag not a מנהג יפה, not a מנהג הגון, and states he himself davens ותיקין every Shabbos. ↩
    Standard reference: שו״ת רדב״ז, likely חלק א or ב (the exact siman was not cited in the shiur). The teshuvah is well-known and quoted by later אחרונים.
  12. גמרא מגילה כ״ג ע״א with רש״י — יום טוב late, Shabbos early (ותיקין) — The גמרא במסכת מגילה דף כ״ג ע״א discusses the timing of תפילת ציבור on various days. On יום טוב, the tzibbur davens later — the reason (per רש״י שם) is that Yom Tov permits cooking, and the community wakes early to prepare the seudah, delaying shul. On Shabbos, when nothing may be cooked and all preparations are complete before shabbos begins, there is no such distraction — one should come to shul early. Rashi comments explicitly: on Shabbos one should daven ותיקין — at sunrise. This reading cuts directly against the מרדכי's late-davening tradition (fn10) and supports the רדב״ז's personal practice (fn12). ↩
  13. Alternative — שֵׁינָה בְּשַׁבָּת תַּעֲנוּג; the pasuk-argument is אַסְמַכְתָּא — Many אחרונים prefer a simpler reading of the late-Shabbos-morning davening: it reflects the halachic category of "שֵׁינָה בְּשַׁבָּת תַּעֲנוּג" — sleep on Shabbos is itself an עונג שבת. On this reading, the pasuk-based argument from Rav Hai Gaon is an אַסְמַכְתָּא — a textual anchor for a pre-existing rationale — rather than the true reason. The מנהג exists because Shabbos is a day of rest; the community extends the sleep period as an honor to the day. The pasuk merely hangs the practice on a textual peg. This reading is echoed in many אחרונים discussions of the timing question. ↩
    The specific אחרונים citing שֵׁינָה בְּשַׁבָּת תַּעֲנוּג as the underlying reason are widespread; the Rav did not cite a specific אחרון by name in the shiur.
  14. Alternative Rishon — שנים מקרא ואחד תרגום before davening — A different Rishon reads the מנהג of later Shabbos-morning davening as an accommodation for learning, not sleep. The oylam wakes at the regular time and uses the intervening hours to complete the weekly obligation of שנים מקרא ואחד תרגום — reading each pasuk of the weekly פרשה twice in the Hebrew original and once in Onkelos — which must be completed by the time the tzibbur reaches the parsha in kriyas hatorah. On this reading, the later davening is a gift only to those who use the time for תורה. The מנהג loses its היתר if one simply sleeps in without learning. ↩
    The Rav referred to "a different version of the Rishonim" without naming the specific Rishon. This reading is associated with several Rishonim in the tradition; a common attribution is to the שיבולי הלקט or ראש, but the Rav did not specify.
  15. הרב ישראל בעלסקי זצ״ל — 9:00 AM as post-war chassidishe innovation — הרב ישראל בעלסקי זצ״ל (1938–2016), rosh yeshiva of Torah Vodaas and posek for the OU, was the Rav's wife's grandfather. The Rav recounted a personal Shabbos in Miami when Rav Belsky expressed strong disapproval of the 9:00 AM Shabbos-morning norm. His claim: this practice emerged post-war and is essentially a חסידישע accommodation that spread to the general Orthodox world. In pre-war old Williamsburg, other than a few chassidishe rebbelach, no minyan started later than 8:15 or 8:20. The concern is not aesthetic but halachic: a 9:00 AM start regularly runs against סוף זמן קריאת שמע and sometimes even סוף זמן תפילה. The Rav noted that while he does not fully agree with Rav Belsky's historical claim (there is some evidence of pre-war 9:00 AM מנינים), the halachic caution about זמנים stands. ↩
  16. מ״ב בשם האר״י ז״ל — פסוקי דזמרה בְלַחַשׁ during week, בְקוֹל וּבְנִיגוּן on Shabbos — The מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה (ר' ישראל מאיר הכהן, the חפץ חיים, 1838–1933), in his discussion of the laws of פסוקי דזמרה (סימן נ״א and following), brings from the אריז״ל a distinction between the volume of פסוקי דזמרה during the week and on Shabbos. During the week: בְלַחַשׁ — quietly. On Shabbos: aloud, with נִיגוּן — expansively and joyfully. The distinction is rooted in the אריז״ל's kabbalistic treatment of Shabbos as the day of expanded שירה, tied to the seventh-wing framework of fn20. In practice, this is most visibly preserved in חסידישע shuls where the entire פסוקי דזמרה of Shabbos morning is sung. ↩
  17. מ״ב — protesting the שליח ציבור for taking long is prohibited even לשם שמים — The מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה writes explicitly that if the שליח ציבור is being מַאֲרִיךְ in פסוקי דזמרה — taking his time, singing, davening with כוונה — a member of the tzibbur is not permitted to protest and pressure him to speed up, even if the protester's motivation is לשם שמים — even if the concern is ביטול תורה from the extra minutes lost. The עיקר הדין is that פסוקי דזמרה should be said בְנַחַת וּבְכוונה. Shabbos gives extra time and the שליח ציבור is halachically entitled to use it. ↩
    Specific siman/סעיף in משנה ברורה was not cited in the shiur — the ruling appears in the general discussion of פסוקי דזמרה in סימנים נ״א–נ״ג.
  18. מ״ב — late-arrival priorities on Shabbos morning — The מִשְׁנָה בְּרוּרָה gives an explicit hierarchy for one who arrives late to Shabbos morning davening and needs to catch up: (1) בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר — the opening ברכה of פסוקי דזמרה; (2) אַשְׁרֵי (תהילים קמ״ה) — the halachically central פרק of the section; (3) יִשְׁתַּבַּח — the closing ברכה; (4) נִשְׁמַת כָּל חַי — the Shabbos-specific centerpiece of the extended שירה. The משנה ברורה is explicit that the extra קפּיטלַך added on Shabbos are not מעכב — they may be omitted if time is tight and made up later if desired. But נִשְׁמַת is not to be missed. It is the central Shabbos-specific piece of the entire שירה of the morning. ↩
  19. זוהר הקדוש (via תוספות סנהדרין ל״ז) — the seventh wing — תוספות במסכת סנהדרין דף ל״ז brings a זוהר / midrashic tradition on the wings of the שרפים. The pasuk in ישעיהו ו׳:ב describes: "שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם לְאֶחָד" — six wings per שרף. Each wing corresponds to one day of the week and is the vehicle for that day's שירה. Sunday one wing, Monday another, through the six weekdays. On Shabbos, the מלאכים have no wing left — they have "used up" all six. They ask הקב״ה: who will sing today? The answer: כנסת ישראל — Klal Yisrael is the seventh wing. Shabbos is "אוֹת הִיא בֵּינִי וּבֵין בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל" — הקב״ה prefers the שירה of His children on this day over the שירה of the מלאכים. The added Shabbos-morning מזמורים are, structurally, the שירה of the מלאכים now sung by Klal Yisrael. ↩
  20. ישעיהו ו׳:ב — "שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם לְאֶחָד" — Full pasuk: "שְׂרָפִים עֹמְדִים מִמַּעַל לוֹ, שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם לְאֶחָד; בִּשְׁתַּיִם יְכַסֶּה פָנָיו וּבִשְׁתַּיִם יְכַסֶּה רַגְלָיו וּבִשְׁתַּיִם יְעוֹפֵף." — "Serafim stood above Him, six wings, six wings to each; with two he covered his face, with two he covered his feet, and with two he would fly." This is the מקור-פסוק for the seventh-wing chiddush of fn20. ↩
  21. בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and יִשְׁתַּבַּח as a בְּרָכָה מַסְגֶּרֶת — בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר functions as the opening ברכה of פסוקי דזמרה, and יִשְׁתַּבַּח functions as its closing ברכה. Together they form a בְּרָכָה מַסְגֶּרֶת — a "framing ברכה" — that brackets the entire פסוקי דזמרה unit as a single halachic entity. The parallel is to a ברכה before eating: just as one may not speak between the ברכה of the food and the eating itself (an interruption between the ברכה and its fulfillment), one may not speak between בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and יִשְׁתַּבַּח. שיחת חולין is prohibited; only דבר מצוה or צורך גדול is permitted. This framework is codified in שולחן ערוך אורח חיים סימן נ״א. ↩
  22. Practical נוסח difference — where בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר begins — נוסח אשכנז and נוסח ספרד/חסידי place בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר at different points in the morning davening on Shabbos. Nusach Ashkenaz begins בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר early — soon after the opening הודו and the introductory pesukim. From that point through יִשְׁתַּבַּח, the no-talking rule applies. Nusach Sfard/Chassidi adds many additional מזמורים before בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר — the קרבנות section, the extra Shabbos morning פרקים, then בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר — creating a longer pre-framework window in which talking is technically permitted (though still discouraged by the general character of a shul setting). The Rav noted this as a common source of confusion — the pre-בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר window in Nusach Sfard is not a designated schmooze-time, only a stretch where the strict בְּרָכָה מַסְגֶּרֶת rule has not yet engaged. ↩
  23. תהילים ל״ד — "לְדָוִד בְּשַׁנּוֹתוֹ אֶת טַעְמוֹ לִפְנֵי אֲבִימֶלֶךְ" — The פרק's superscription references the narrative in שמואל א' פרק כ״א. After defeating גָּלְיָת (I Sam 17) and increasing tension with Shaul (I Sam 18–19), David fled and eventually reached the court of אָכִישׁ מֶלֶךְ גַּת. There, recognized as the warrior about whom the streets sang, David was in mortal danger. He feigned madness ("וַיְשַׁנּוֹ אֶת־טַעְמוֹ בְּעֵינֵיהֶם וַיִּתְהֹלֵל בְּיָדָם וַיְתָו עַל־דַּלְתוֹת הַשַּׁעַר וַיּוֹרֶד רִירוֹ אֶל־זְקָנוֹ" — I Sam 21:14) — drooled, marked the doorposts, played the lunatic. Achish dismissed him: "חֲסֵר מְשֻׁגָּעִים אָנִי כִּי־הֲבֵאתֶם אֶת־זֶה לְהִשְׁתַּגֵּעַ עָלָי?" — "am I short of madmen, that you have brought this one to be crazy at me?" (I Sam 21:16). The פרק of תהילים ל״ד is David's שיר of הודאה composed after that deliverance: "אֲבָרְכָה אֶת ה' בְּכָל עֵת; תָּמִיד תְּהִלָּתוֹ בְּפִי". ↩
    The superscription of the פרק uses "Avimelech" while the shmuel narrative uses "Achish." חז״ל (see מדרש תהילים on פרק ל״ד) explain that "Avimelech" is a general title for the kings of Plishtim, analogous to "Paro" for Egypt — the actual king was Achish.
  24. חז״ל — Achish's wife and daughter were already משוגעים — חז״ל on the passuk in Shmuel (see מדרש שמואל and citations in the מפרשים on I Sam 21) note that Achish's own household included two active משוגעים — his wife and his daughter — screaming through the palace continually. Achish's dismissal of David — "am I short of madmen?" — was not a mere idiom but reflected a domestic reality: he had too many. When David began carrying on in the courtyard, Achish snapped that he already had a headache from his own and had no room for another, and expelled him. That mundane household detail is what tipped the scales that saved David's life. This is a common thread in the מפרשים on the sugya: הקב״ה arranged the historical circumstances such that David's ruse would land. ↩
    The specific חז״ל/midrashic source was cited by the Rav from oral tradition; standard reference is מדרש שמואל on פרק כ״א and citations in later מפרשים (Malbim, Rashi on the pesukim).
  25. The Shabbos connection — why תהילים ל״ד belongs on Shabbos morning — The Rav's chiddush (or the reading he preserved from his mesorah): the deliverance of David before Achish took place on Shabbos. That is the reason many נוסחאות include תהילים ל״ד among the Shabbos morning additions. The פרק is דָּוִד's שיר של הודאה composed after that Shabbos-day deliverance, and its central pasuk — "אֲבָרְכָה אֶת ה' בְּכָל עֵת; תָּמִיד תְּהִלָּתוֹ בְּפִי" — is a שירת הודאה born of vulnerability and אמונה under pressure. Hashkafically this fits the seventh-wing framework of fn20: הקב״ה prefers on Shabbos not the polished שירה of the מלאכים at their strongest, but the שירה of Klal Yisrael from a position of חסרון. David feigning madness before Achish is the mashal for exactly this — greatness expressed through vulnerability. ↩
    The Rav stated this Shabbos-connection reading; the exact source of the tradition that the deliverance occurred specifically on Shabbos was not cited in the shiur. Standard מפרשים on I Sam 21 do not make the Shabbos claim explicit, though the association of תהילים ל״ד with Shabbos morning davening is a widespread מנהג. Worth verifying against later chassidishe / mussar sources.
