Rabbi Ephrati · Sunday Morning שיעור on תפילה

Toledo's שָׂמַחְתִּי, Adam HaRishon's Sunrise, and the מחזור ויטרי's נשמת Rebuttal

The Additions to Shabbos פסוקי דזמרה — a Toledo synagogue in 1180, the first Shabbos morning ever, and a Rishonic thundering against a myth
Recorded 16 August 2026 · ג׳ אלול תשפ״ו · Reworked from the original שיעור

This is the second week on שחרית של שבת. Last week set the map — additions and omissions, the מזמור לתודה machloikes, the Zohar's seventh wing, the timing question. This week goes into the specific added מזמורים: why נוסח ספרד places its additions before ברוך שאמר (a hidden accommodation for latecomers); a Rishonic chiddush that these additions were originally daily until practical necessity curtailed them; and three named stories with real texture — a Toledo synagogue built in 1180 by רבנו יוסף בן שושן that gave us שמחתי באמרים לי; the first sunrise Adam HaRishon ever saw after the חטא, when he composed מזמור שיר ליום השבת; and the מחזור ויטרי's furious rebuttal of the legend that נשמת was written by an undercover Jew who infiltrated early Christianity.

I. ג׳ אלול — Opening the Second Week

The Rav opened noting today's יארצייט — רבי יהודה הלוי (author of the Kuzari), with tonight being the יארצייט of the אור שמח (רבי מאיר שמחה הכהן of Dvinsk). זכותם יגן עלינו. We continue the arc from last week: שחרית של שבת, comparing and contrasting פסוקי דזמרה of Shabbos to פסוקי דזמרה of the weekday, drilling now into the specific added מזמורים and their sources.

II. The ברכה מסגרת Recalled

בָּרוּךְ שֶׁאָמַר and יִשְׁתַּבַּח form a בְּרָכָה מַסְגֶּרֶת[1] around פסוקי דזמרה. The opening ברכה declares: we are about to spend ten or fifteen minutes praising הקב״ה with the words of שירי דוד עבדך נהללך. The closing ברכה seals that praise. Therefore, the Rishonim ruled, all additions of Shabbos morning — the extra קפּיטלַך תהילים — must sit inside the frame, between ברוך שאמר and ישתבח. That is their structural home; they are, literally, part of the praise the opening ברכה promised.

This drives Nusach Ashkenaz's practice: ברוך שאמר is said early, immediately after the opening pesukim; from that point through ישתבח, no talking, and the Shabbos additions live inside the bracket. Nusach Sfard/חסידי does something structurally different — the additions come before ברוך שאמר, which on its face contradicts the framework principle.

III. Why Nusach Sfard Puts the Additions Before ברוך שאמר — an Accommodation Hidden in Plain Sight

The Rav pressed a point he has emphasized many times across this series: חז״ל and the מסדרי הסידור were consistently sensitive to latecomers.[2] Not encouraging lateness — but repeatedly building the structure of תפילה so that a Yid who arrives late can still be יוצא the essentials. This is not a modern accommodation; it is a Rishonic sensibility.

ברוך שאמר is one of those essentials. In earlier generations, many congregants did not know how to daven independently — they relied on the שליח ציבור's recitation of ברוך שאמר and were יוצא via שומע כעונה. If ברוך שאמר is said too early on Shabbos morning — before the tzibbur has fully arrived — many will miss it entirely, with no way to be יוצא.

Therefore Nusach Sfard's structural inversion: begin with הודו and the extended קפּיטלַך, giving the tzibbur ten or fifteen minutes to gather; only then say ברוך שאמר, catching everyone who has by now arrived. The ideal structural framework yields to the practical need. Nusach Ashkenaz, by contrast, prioritizes the framework and accepts that latecomers miss ברוך שאמר — perhaps because, as the Rav quipped, Yekkes don't come late.

IV. Three Reasons Why More מזמורים on Shabbos

The First Two — Reviewed from Last Week

Tosfos, סנהדרין דף ל״ז[3] brings the Zohar of the six wings — מלאכי השרת have six wings each, one used for שירה each day of the week. On Shabbos, no wing remains; כלל ישראל becomes the seventh wing. Our extra מזמורים are the שירה of the מלאכים, sung by us.

הטור[4] gives a simpler reason: tircha d'tzibbura. During the week the oylam is pressed — work, appointments — and חז״ל were reluctant to make davening long. On Shabbos, we have time. The added קפּיטלַך reflect that opportunity, and the honor of the day.

The Third — a Startling Chiddush from רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא

A striking chiddush from רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא[5] — R' Shlomo of Worms, a late-11th / early-12th century Rishon (roughly a contemporary of Rashi), a major mekubal and tzaddik. In his own סידור, he writes that the original תקנה of פסוקי דזמרה included the full expanded Shabbos corpus every single day. Somewhere along Jewish history — no clear timeline — the Rishonim or later authorities recognized that the daily oylam could not sustain such long davening. People were leaving early to get to work; some were missing אשרי ובא לציון. Practical necessity trumped the ideal, and the extended set was curtailed for weekdays, preserved fully only for Shabbos.

רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא then adds: והאומרם כל יום תבא עליו ברכה — one who says them daily is blessed. The Rav noted, wryly, that he doesn't know a single Yid today who follows this. But it establishes the חשיבות of what we do say on Shabbos: it is not liturgical padding. It is the original ideal template of daily פסוקי דזמרה, preserved intact on the one day that permits it.

An Aside — the Otisville Story The Rav paused for a small mashal from his friend Tzvi Tress, son of Mike Tress. Tzvi once visited a chaver in Otisville prison (there is a frum minyan there). He davened Shacharis with the inmates and saw a man begin taking off his tefillin during אשרי ובא לציון. He couldn't help himself: Where are you going? You're stuck here! We have an excuse — we need to get to work — but you have all day! The Rav connected it: this is what drove the historical shortening of daily פסוקי דזמרה — the oylam needed to leave. The instinct to shorten is old.

V. שָׂמַחְתִּי בְּאֹמְרִים לִי — a Chiddush from a Single Toledo Synagogue in 1180

The Puzzle

שיר המעלות שָׂמַחְתִּי בְּאֹמְרִים לִי בֵּית ה' נֵלֵךְ — תהילים קכ״ב[6] — appears in every Nusach Sfard siddur for Shabbos morning. Yet — the Rav noted with real puzzlement — no Rishon includes this פרק in the Shabbos-morning list. Not the Abudraham, not מחזור ויטרי, not רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא. Every other kapitel we add has a Rishonic source. Why is this one uniformly recited every Shabbos worldwide, yet unattested in the standard Rishonim?

The One Source — the ספר המנהיג

There is one Rishon who records the origin: the ספר המנהיג[7], by רבי אברהם בן נתן הירחי (early 13th century). And the story he preserves is remarkable in specificity.

In the year 1180, in Toledo, Spain, a Jew named רבנו יוסף בן שושן completed the construction of a grand synagogue.[8] The Ibn Shushan family were the נשיאים of the Toledo Jewish community — recognized by the government, extraordinarily wealthy. רבי יוסף himself was a גאון, צדיק, ומקובל — and served as treasurer/finance minister to King Alfonso VIII of Castile. Something like the position דון יצחק אברבנאל would later hold under Ferdinand and Isabella.

Driven by his devotion, רבי יוסף built not merely a shul but a massive complex — a מקדש מעט of astonishing scale for the age.

On the first Shabbos morning that the tzibbur gathered to daven in the newly inaugurated shul, רבי יוסף stood before them — overwhelmed by the joy and sanctity of the moment — and declared that in honor of this new בית ה', they would add one specific פרק to the Shabbos-morning פסוקי דזמרה: שָׂמַחְתִּי בְּאֹמְרִים לִי בֵּית ה' נֵלֵךְ. The name of the shul, the joy of "בית ה'", the pasuk — a triple echo.

The ספר המנהיג records: הנשיא רבנו יהוסף בנה בית מפואר. The custom was adopted by other Toledan communities. Through the influence of the ספר המנהיג as a widely-read digest of minhagim, it spread outward. And today, millions of Yidden worldwide say שָׂמַחְתִּי every Shabbos morning — because of that single evening in Toledo, in 1180. The Rav paused on the observation: you can imagine millions of Jews worldwide saying a kapitel because of that one story.

The Gemara's Backstory — Why David Rejoiced

The pasuk itself carries a Gemara backstory that deepens what the ibn Shushan meant. The גמרא במסכת מכות דף י'[9] teaches: דוד המלך heard murmurings behind his back. People were saying: מתי ימות זקן זה ונבנה בית המקדש? — "When will this old man die, so the Beis HaMikdash can finally be built?" The oylam knew that Dovid was told he could not build the Mikdash himself — "יָדֶיךָ דָּמִים מָלֵאוּ", hands full of blood, even the blood of Golyus and the Plishtim, disqualified him. Only Shlomo, his son, would build.

The natural response to such murmuring would be insult, even rage. Dovid's response was שָׂמַחְתִּי — "I rejoiced." Why? Because the murmuring itself revealed the כיסופין of כלל ישראל — a burning collective desire to be עולה לרגל, to serve in בית ה'. Yes, the words were harsh — a שטיקל חוצפה — but under the harshness was a national longing. Dovid heard past the disrespect to what it revealed.

When רבי יוסף בן שושן chose this specific pasuk for the inauguration of his shul in Toledo, he was pointing to that same layered joy: we build a בית ה' not because we have arrived, but because we long for the one that is not yet built. This shul is a מקדש מעט that carries the yearning forward.

VI. The Toledo Synagogue's Later History — Vincent Ferrer and Santa Maria la Blanca

The Rav closed the arc with the shul's own history. The building still stands today — the oldest surviving synagogue in Europe. But not as a shul.[10]

In 1391 — a full century before the 1492 expulsion under Ferdinand and Isabella — a wave of anti-Jewish violence and forced conversions swept Christian Spain (the "1391 pogroms," initiated in Seville by the preacher Ferrand Martínez and spreading to Toledo and beyond). The Dominican friar Vincent Ferrer — later canonized — is the figure most associated in Jewish memory with this campaign; his own itinerant preaching-tours peaked in the early 1400s and directly targeted synagogues. He would enter a shul with a cross in one hand and a ספר תורה in the other — he was learned, knew תורה שבכתב and much of תורה שבעל פה — and declare the building a church. Many thousands of Sephardic Jews were converted (or killed) across the combined events of 1391 and Ferrer's later ministry.

He came to the ibn Shushan synagogue in Toledo. Within a few years the Catholic Church took it over and renamed it Sinagoga de Santa María la Blanca. The name is jarring on the tongue — להבדיל אלף אלפי הבדלות. (The Rav noted in passing that the Satmar Rov used to sign אב בית דין דקהילה קדושה סאקמאר with a קוף instead of טית, believing the town's name was somehow connected to "Saint Mary" — the historical linkage is now understood to be a mistake, but the discomfort was real.)

The building served as a church for over six centuries. Only in 2013 did the Archbishop of Toledo formally return it to the local Jewish community. Today it functions as a Jewish museum. It is one of the most ancient standing synagogue buildings in Europe (some argue the oldest continuously standing; the Old-New Synagogue in Prague, c. 1270, is a competitor for the same title).

The architecture is striking: mudéjar — designed by Muslim architects working for Jewish patrons in Christian Spain. Small windows, distinctive arches, a Magen David preserved in the mizrach wall, a central arch where the Aron Kodesh once stood. Photos are online for anyone curious.

VII. Who Was רבי אברהם הירחי?

A word on the source. רבי אברהם בן נתן הירחי[11] — the author of ספר המנהיג — is an unusual Rishon. Most Rishonim were born, learned, taught, and died within one region — Ashkenaz, or Sfarad, or Italy, or Provence. Their vantage was local; their halachic decisions reflected the practice of their own community.

Not so רבי אברהם הירחי. He was "נע ונד" — a wandering Jew. He was born in Lunel (לוֹנֵל) in Provence, France — the name "Hairchi" (הירחי) meaning "of the moon" (Lune in French, ירח in Hebrew). He then traveled by choice, systematically, kehilla to kehilla — France, Germany, Provence — documenting the differences between local מנהגים. He was, in modern language, an ethnographer of מנהגים.

He ended his life in Toledo, where he died around 1215 CE. It is precisely his wandering, his willingness to travel to Spain and document Toledan practices, that preserves for us the story of the ibn Shushan synagogue and the addition of שמחתי. Had he stayed in Provence, we would have no source for this custom, and every Yid today would say שמחתי Shabbos morning with no idea why.

VIII. הַלֵּל הַגָּדוֹל — Why the "Great" הַלֵּל

We move on to תהילים קל״ו: הוֹדוּ לַה' כִּי טוֹב כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ — הַלֵּל הַגָּדוֹל, the "Great Hallel." Why great? The גמרא במסכת פסחים דף קי״ח[12] gives Rabbi Yochanan's answer: because הקב״ה sits in the heights of His world and personally distributes sustenance to every creature. The proof-text is the pasuk toward the end: נֹתֵן לֶחֶם לְכָל בָּשָׂר, כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ — "He gives food to all flesh, for His kindness is everlasting."

The גדלות of הקב״ה is not the majesty of the abstract sovereign. It is the specific, granular care that ensures every ant, every fish, every bird, every human being has what to eat. In infinite majesty, He is worried about the smallest בריה. That is why this פרק — praising exactly that quality — is called great.

The גמרא בברכות דף ד'[13] even records a hava amina — a passing consideration — to require הַלֵּל הַגָּדוֹל as a daily obligation, in place of אַשְׁרֵי. Both share the alphabetic-acrostic feature that Chazal valued; both praise הקב״ה broadly. The Gemara ultimately kept אַשְׁרֵי as the daily standard — but the fact that הַלֵּל הַגָּדוֹל was even considered for daily recitation confirms its status.

We say it on Shabbos morning and on Pesach night at the Seder — both times when the theme of בריאת העולם and universal חסד is central.

The Chasam Sofer's Kugel Joke The פרק contains twenty-six refrains of כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ. The חתם סופר is said to have shared a joke about this. He was once traveling and stayed by a simple, unlearned Yid — a פּוֹער, a peasant. Friday night, the host proudly brought out twenty-four different kugels — potato, spinach, broccoli, every kind. Chasam Sofer asked why so many. The host replied: because on Shabbos we say כי לעולם חסדו twenty-four times, so each kugel is כנגד one. Chasam Sofer corrected him: there are twenty-six. The host, without missing a beat: oh — לסיחון מלך האמורי and ולעוג מלך הבשן don't deserve a kugel.

IX. הָאַדֶּרֶת וְהָאֱמוּנָה — A Prayer of the מלאכים, with Napoleon's March

Some קהילות — particularly חסידישע — recite הָאַדֶּרֶת וְהָאֱמוּנָה in Shabbos פסוקי דזמרה: an alphabetical acrostic of divine attributes, drawn from פרקי היכלות — the mystical literature ascribing שירה to the מלאכים.[14]

The מהרי״ל ruled that this תפילה is so heilig it should not be said בציבור except on Yom Kippur — the one day of the year when Klal Yisrael's status as מלאכים is halachically formalized (שבת שבתון, no eating, no drinking, white bigadim). On any other day, saying the שירה of the מלאכים risks being a חוצפה.

The חסידישע oylam nonetheless recites it every Shabbos. The justification: the Rav pointed to a parallel to what he taught last week about the seventh wing (Section IV above) — on Shabbos, a Yid stands on the מדרגה of a מלאך. If Klal Yisrael on Shabbos is the seventh wing of the שרפים, then a Yid on Shabbos can say the שירה that on ordinary days belongs only to the מלאכים.

Some חסידים nonetheless preserve the מהרי״ל's concern by saying הָאַדֶּרֶת before the formal beginning of דאווענען — so it is not technically part of the tzibbur's tefillah. Chabad, famously, sings it to a ניגון traditionally attributed to Napoleon Bonaparte's march — described as "the song of the מלאכים."

The Rav noted: שבת may in a certain sense carry a קדושה even greater than Yom Kippur. Yom Kippur is called שבת שבתון — Shabbos-of-Shabboses — but the base kedusha, the source, is Shabbos itself. So the very argument for why we should not say הָאַדֶּרֶת except on Yom Kippur may quietly justify saying it on Shabbos too.

X. מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת — Adam HaRishon's First Sunrise

The Song of Shabbos

The most important addition on Shabbos morning is מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת — תהילים צ״ב[15]. This was the שיר של יום that the לויים would sing in the בית המקדש when the תמיד של שחר was brought on Shabbos. Every day the לויים sang a different Shir Shel Yom; on Shabbos, this פרק.

Who composed it originally? אבות דרבי נתן[16] answers: אָדָם הָרִאשׁוֹן. And the story behind that composition is one of the most moving moments in the entire Torah-she-b'al-peh treatment of תפילה.

The First Sunrise Ever

אדם הראשון was created on Friday. On Friday afternoon he committed the חטא עץ הדעת. As Shabbos began, the sun started to set — for the first time in his experience. This was the first day of a human being's life on earth, and therefore, in the most literal sense, the first night.

As darkness fell, אדם was overcome with terror. His interpretation of the setting sun was catastrophic: oy va'avoy — I sinned, and now the world will be plunged into eternal darkness. This is it. The world is going to implode. The world would end, in the dark, because of him.

The ריטב״א על עבודה זרה[17] raises the obvious difficulty. אדם הראשון was, per the Gemara's descriptions, the wisest being ever created — a person of profound spiritual insight. Did he not understand that there is a natural rhythm of ויהי ערב ויהי בוקר, evening and morning? Even without direct experience, surely his intellect grasped the cyclical structure of creation.

The Ritva's answer: of course he understood. But החטא מבלבלו — the sin disorients. Post-חטא, his clear mind clouded. He could not believe that the world would continue. The אבות דרבי נתן continues: he stayed awake the entire night, waiting to see whether morning would come. The wisest being in creation, reduced by his own חטא to hoping desperately that dawn would break.

The Sunrise

Shabbos morning, the sun rose. אדם saw the light and understood: Ribono Shel Olam — the world continues. Even though I sinned. Even though I broke the first commandment ever given. A person can fail — and there is still a morning.

Overcome, he composed מִזְמוֹר שִׁיר לְיוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת, טוֹב לְהוֹדוֹת לַה' — a song of thanks for the day of Shabbos, for the day whose sunrise revealed that the world was not over. And then: לְהַגִּיד בַּבֹּקֶר חַסְדֶּךָ וֶאֱמוּנָתְךָ בַּלֵּילוֹת — to declare Your kindness in the morning, and Your faithfulness in the nights. The night was terrifying; the morning declared חסד.

The אבות דרבי נתן adds a stunning detail: at the moment אדם began to sing, "באותה שעה ירדו כיתות של מלאכי השרת" — kittos of מלאכים descended from Shamayim, with musical instruments in hand: עֲלֵי עָשׂוֹר וַעֲלֵי נָבֶל, עֲלֵי הִגָּיוֹן בְּכִנּוֹר — with the ten-string, with the נבל, with the הגיון and the כנור — the very instruments the פרק itself names. Heavenly music at the first human sunrise. Every Shabbos when we recite מזמור שיר ליום השבת, we should be filled with the אמונה that the sun always rises again — that a Yid who fails can always begin again.

Naftali's Question — שביתת כלים

A talmid — Naftali — raised a striking question: how could the מלאכים play instruments on Shabbos? Isn't there an issue of שביתת כלים (the halachic prohibition on one's utensils performing melacha on Shabbos)? Are the מלאכים מחלל שבת?

The Rav appreciated the question — quoting the pasuk "נַפְתָּלִי אַיָּלָה שְׁלוּחָה הַנֹּתֵן אִמְרֵי־שָׁפֶר" (בראשית מ״ט:כ״א) — and added a further wrinkle: אבות דרבי נתן also records that אדם brought קרבנות that morning. But קרבן יחיד is not brought on Shabbos (per last week's shiur on מזמור לתודה)! Some Rishonim resolve this by saying the קרבנות were brought only Sunday, not Shabbos itself. The מלאכים's instruments — perhaps also. The full resolution the Rav left open — the question is asked, the sugya is deeper.

XI. ה' מָלָךְ גֵּאוּת לָבֵשׁ — הקב״ה's בגדי שבת

Immediately after מזמור שיר ליום השבת (פרק צ״ב), we add תהילים צ״ג[18]: ה' מָלָךְ, גֵּאוּת לָבֵשׁ — לָבֵשׁ ה' עֹז הִתְאַזָּר — Hashem has reigned; He has clothed Himself in majesty. Why this פרק, specifically for Shabbos?

A striking chiddush from חז״ל. Just as we put on בגדי שבת — as we say Friday night, קִצַּתִּי מַטָּתִי וְהֶחֱלַפְתִּי שִׂמְלוֹתַי לִכְבוֹד יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת — so, כביכול, הקב״ה "puts on" the קדושה of Shabbos as a garment. The Friday-night piyut לק-ל אשר שבת[19] makes this explicit: "תִּפְאֶרֶת עָטָה לְיוֹם הַמְּנוּחָה" — He wrapped Himself in תפארת for the day of rest.

When we put on our own בגדי שבת, we should know we are emulating a divine act. The kavod we wear is a mimicry of the kavod הקב״ה "wears." ה' מלך גאות לבש — the pasuk that commemorates this — is added on Shabbos morning to say: the קדושה of Shabbos is in all the עולמות. Even in Shamayim, something changes on Shabbos, and we align ourselves to it in the material act of putting on nicer clothes.

XII. נִשְׁמַת כָּל חַי — The Rebuttal of a Painful Legend

The Yesod v'Shoresh of Shabbos Morning

נִשְׁמַת כָּל חַי is the peak of Shabbos-morning דאווענען — the יסוד ושורש העבודה of Shabbos שחרית per the great מקובלים. It is a segulah in its own right (some women recite it daily). But the question of authorship carries a painful history that requires being addressed openly.

The Legend of Shimon Kefa

די וועלט זאגט — the "common talk" says — that נשמת does not come from an entirely "kosher" source. The legend involves a Jew named Shimon Kefa (Shimon "Kefa" — Peter, in the Latin/Greek transliteration).[20]

The historical background: in the era of the תנאים, when the movement following Yoshke (ימח שמו וזכרו) first emerged, the early Christians were not a separate community. They were Yidden — the same neighbors, the same shul-goers, the same friends who went to the mikvah on Friday afternoon. There was no clear line. The תנאים understood that without a clear separation between the two religions — one Torah, one heresy — Klal Yisrael would face a mess of confusion.

According to the legend, the תנאים officially appointed Shimon Kefa — a frum Yid — as an undercover agent. His mission: infiltrate the early Christian movement, rise through the ranks, and establish laws for the Christians that would create a definitive separation from Yiddishkeit. He is credited (per this legend) with instituting Sunday as the Christian day of rest — a single stroke that made the Shabbos-boundary between the two religions permanent and unmistakable.

If this legend is true, and if the same Shimon Kefa returned to Klal Yisrael and composed נשמת — what business does it have in our siddur?

The מחזור ויטרי's Thunder

The מחזור ויטרי[21] — one of the great Rishonim, authored by ר' שמחה בן שמואל מויטרי, a talmid of Rashi — heard this rumor and wrote against it with unusual sharpness. His formulation, in the Rav's paraphrase: anyone who claims נשמת was written by this Yid who became a Christian — even if undercover — will be חייב, when the Beis HaMikdash is rebuilt, to bring a קרבן חטאת שמנה for uttering such a falsehood about a holy tefillah.

חס ושלום מלהזכיר. The מחזור ויטרי brings multiple רָאיוֹת that נשמת was composed by the אַנְשֵׁי כְּנֶסֶת הַגְּדוֹלָה — the standard authorship attributed to the fixed parts of our siddur. The tefillah is heilig; its provenance is Anshei Knesses HaGedola; the legend is false and its utterance is a sin.

What to Make of the Rebuttal

The Rav's tone was measured — this is a painful sugya. He did not endorse the legend, and he did not soften the מחזור ויטרי's thunder. The point of raising it is that the Rishonim themselves felt the need to address it — meaning the rumor was in circulation among Jews in Northern France in Rashi's era, and it required a strong response. That the מחזור ויטרי chose to invoke a חטאת שמנה — an unusually severe formulation — measures how seriously he took the potential damage of the legend. When you say נשמת, you are saying words the אנשי כנסת הגדולה composed. That is the fact of the matter per one of Rashi's closest talmidim.

XIV. Practical Takeaways

  • The ברכה מסגרת: from ברוך שאמר through ישתבח, no talking. Nusach-specific window opens at different points; the rule is the same.
  • שמחתי: next time you say it, remember Toledo, 1180, and the ibn Shushan synagogue's first Shabbos.
  • Hallel HaGadol: the greatness is the granular care that feeds every creature. Say it with that in mind.
  • מזמור שיר ליום השבת: Adam HaRishon's first sunrise. A Yid who fell yesterday can rise today. The world does not end at nightfall.
  • ה' מלך: when you put on בגדי שבת, know you are emulating what הקב״ה is doing kaviyachol.
  • נשמת: composed by the אנשי כנסת הגדולה per the מחזור ויטרי. The Shimon Kefa legend is false and its repetition is severely warned against.

  1. ברוך שאמר and ישתבח as ברכה מסגרת — The framework doctrine is standard in the Rishonim on פסוקי דזמרה and is codified in שולחן ערוך אורח חיים סימן נ״א (opening ברכה) and סימן נ״ד (closing ברכה). Just as one may not speak between the ברכה on food and eating the food, one may not speak between ברוך שאמר and ישתבח. שיחת חולין is prohibited; only דבר מצוה or צורך גדול is permitted. Extensively developed in last week's shiur (2026-08-09). ↩
  2. Latecomer accommodation — שומע כעונה via the שליח ציבור — The Rav emphasized that Chazal and the מסדרי הסידור repeatedly structured תפילה to accommodate latecomers via שומע כעונה — the halachic principle (see שולחן ערוך אורח חיים סימן ח:י־יא for its general application to ברכות) that one who listens to another's recitation with intent to be included is halachically as though he recited it himself. In earlier generations, many Yidden did not know how to daven independently and relied on the שליח ציבור's recitation of key ברכות. This creates a real halachic weight to timing decisions in tefillah: when ברוך שאמר is said too early on Shabbos morning, those relying on שומע כעונה lose the ברכה entirely. ↩
  3. תוספות סנהדרין דף ל״ז — Zohar of the six wings — תוספות במסכת סנהדרין דף ל״ז brings a זוהר/midrashic tradition on the wings of the שרפים. The pasuk in ישעיהו ו':ב describes: "שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם שֵׁשׁ כְּנָפַיִם לְאֶחָד" — six wings per שרף. Each wing corresponds to one day of the week and serves as the vehicle for that day's שירה. On Shabbos, the מלאכים have no wing left; כלל ישראל becomes the seventh wing. The added Shabbos-morning מזמורים are, structurally, the שירה of the מלאכים now sung by Klal Yisrael. See last week's shiur (2026-08-09), Section VIII, for the full development. ↩
  4. הטור — תרחא דצבורא relaxes on Shabbos — The טור, אורח חיים (likely סימן נ״ד or the introductory discussion to פסוקי דזמרה) gives the pragmatic reason for the added קפּיטלַך on Shabbos: during the week, תרחא דצבורא — the burden on a tzibbur that must get to work — constrained Chazal from imposing extended liturgy. On Shabbos, when time is not a constraint, the tzibbur has room for the fuller form of פסוקי דזמרה. This is the "practical" reason; the Tosfos/Zohar answer (fn3) is the "structural" reason. Both may be true simultaneously. ↩
    The Rav cited "הטור" generally without specifying siman. Standard reference: הטור אורח חיים בהלכות פסוקי דזמרה, סימנים נ״א–נ״ד.
  5. רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא — Shabbos פסוקי דזמרה was originally the daily standard — רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא — R' Shlomo of Worms (Germany), roughly 50 years before Rashi (mid-11th c.), a major mekubal and Rishon. He wrote his own סידור. In it he records the striking chiddush that the original תקנה of פסוקי דזמרה was the full expanded set — every day. Over time, communities curtailed the weekday version due to practical constraints (people had to get to work; the extended set was becoming a barrier to attendance). The full expansion was preserved only for Shabbos. He adds: וְהָאוֹמְרָם כָּל יוֹם תָּבֹא עָלָיו בְּרָכָה — one who says the extended set every day is blessed. As the Rav noted, no known contemporary practice follows this ideal daily. But it re-frames what we say on Shabbos: not liturgical padding, but the recovered original template. ↩
    The Rav cited "his סידור" without a specific siman. Standard reference: סידור רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא (published in scholarly edition in modern times; also cited by the מחזור ויטרי and other Ashkenazi Rishonim). The Bishop-Solomon detail (community leader recognized by the non-Jewish authorities) is historically attested for Rishonic Ashkenazi גדולים.
  6. תהילים קכ״ב — שיר המעלות לדוד: שמחתי באמרים לי בית ה' נלך — Full opening pasuk: "שִׁיר הַמַּעֲלוֹת לְדָוִד: שָׂמַחְתִּי בְּאֹמְרִים לִי בֵּית ה' נֵלֵךְ. עֹמְדוֹת הָיוּ רַגְלֵינוּ בִּשְׁעָרַיִךְ יְרוּשָׁלִָם". The פרק is one of the שירי המעלות (Songs of Ascents, פרקים קכ–קלד) traditionally sung by the לויים on the fifteen steps between the עזרת נשים and the עזרת ישראל during the שמחת בית השואבה. It ends: "לְמַעַן אַחַי וְרֵעָי אֲדַבְּרָה נָּא שָׁלוֹם בָּךְ; לְמַעַן בֵּית ה' אֱלֹקֵינוּ אֲבַקְשָׁה טוֹב לָךְ" — famously the beloved song of the late רב קלוגר / רב קליש זצ״ל (referenced by the Rav — sung at his levaya to Reb Shlomo Carlebach's ניגון). ↩
  7. ספר המנהיג — the sole Rishonic source for adding שמחתי on Shabbos — The ספר המנהיג — by רבי אברהם בן נתן הירחי — is a comprehensive digest of Jewish מנהגים collected from the author's travels between kehillos in France, Provence, Germany, and Spain (early 13th century). The Rav emphasized that this is the only Rishonic source for adding שמחתי באמרים לי to Shabbos-morning פסוקי דזמרה. The Abudraham, מחזור ויטרי, and רבנו שלמה מגרמייזא all discuss the Shabbos additions but do not list this פרק. Standard reference: ספר המנהיג, הלכות שבת, סימן כ״ג. ↩
    The Rav framed this as an "invitation to the oylam" to find any other Rishonic source. Worth periodic re-checking as more Rishonim's texts become digitized. Confirmed sole source as of current scholarship.
  8. רבנו יוסף בן שושן — 1180, Toledo synagogue inauguration — Rabbeinu Yosef ibn Shushan (also spelled Ben Shushan) — Rishonic-era Toledan גאון, צדיק, ומקובל. Head of a wealthy Nasi family that carried the title נשיאים — recognized by the Castilian crown as leaders of the Jewish community. He served as treasurer / finance minister to King Alfonso VIII of Castile (r. 1158–1214), analogous to the position דון יצחק אברבנאל would later hold under Ferdinand and Isabella. In 1180, using his personal resources, he constructed the massive synagogue complex that later became known as Santa María la Blanca. On its inaugural Shabbos morning, he instituted the addition of שמחתי באמרים לי to פסוקי דזמרה — a triple echo of the new "בית ה'": the shul's dedication, the joy of the moment, and the pasuk's yearning language. ↩
  9. גמרא מכות דף י' — "מתי ימות זקן זה" and Dovid's joy — The גמרא במסכת מכות דף י' ע״א teaches: דוד המלך heard murmurings from Klal Yisrael — "מתי ימות זקן זה ונבנה בית המקדש?" — "when will this old man die so the Beis HaMikdash can be built?" Rather than being insulted, Dovid rejoiced (שמחתי) — because the very rudeness of the murmuring revealed the depth of Klal Yisrael's yearning to be עולה לרגל. Dovid knew (per נביא — "יָדֶיךָ דָּמִים מָלֵאוּ", דברי הימים א' כ״ב:ח) that he himself would not build the Mikdash — that Shlomo would. The nation understood this too, and their impatience for the Mikdash's construction focused on his lifespan. His response — שמחתי — becomes the theological ground of the entire פרק. ↩
  10. Toledo synagogue's later history — Vincent Ferrer, 1391 → Santa María la Blanca → 2013 return — In 1391, a full century before the Alhambra Decree of 1492, the Dominican friar Vincent Ferrer (later canonized by the Catholic Church, ~1350–1419) led a violent conversion campaign across Christian Spain. Historians estimate tens of thousands of Sephardic Jews were forcibly converted or killed in the summer 1391 pogroms alone. Ferrer entered synagogues holding a cross and a ספר תורה — he had studied תורה שבכתב and חלק of Torah She'be'al Peh in order to argue with Jews on their terms — and declared each shul a church. The Toledo synagogue built by ibn Shushan was among those he took. The Catholic Church renamed it Sinagoga de Santa María la Blanca and used it as a church for over six centuries. In 2013, the Archbishop of Toledo formally returned it to the local Jewish community. It functions today as a Jewish museum — the oldest surviving synagogue building in Europe (~845 years old as of 2026). The Rav noted the striking mudéjar architecture — designed by Muslim architects working for Jewish patrons in Christian Spain — a rare surviving example of the multi-religious cultural context of medieval Toledo. ↩
  11. רבי אברהם בן נתן הירחי — the wandering Rishon — רבי אברהם בן נתן הירחי (c. 1155 – c. 1215) — an unusual Rishon in his geographic mobility. Born in Lunel (לוֹנֵל) in Provence — the epithet הירחי ("of the moon") is a pun on the town name: French lune = Hebrew ירח. He traveled systematically through Provence, Northern France, and Germany, documenting the varying מנהגים of each community. He eventually settled in Toledo, Spain, where he lived his last years and where the ibn Shushan synagogue's first-Shabbos-morning story was preserved directly to him. His ספר המנהיג is one of the most important early-Rishonic sources for the geographic variation of Jewish practice. Contrast: most Rishonim were born, learned, and died within a single region — his willingness to travel is what preserves for us the entire chapter of the שמחתי chiddush. ↩
  12. גמרא פסחים דף קי״ח — why הלל הגדול is called "great" — The גמרא במסכת פסחים דף קי״ח ע״א records Rabbi Yochanan's answer: "אָמַר רַבִּי יוֹחָנָן... מִפְּנֵי שֶׁהַקָּדוֹשׁ בָּרוּךְ הוּא יוֹשֵׁב בְּרוּם עוֹלָמוֹ וּמְחַלֵּק מְזוֹנוֹת לְכׇל בְּרִיָּה" — "because הקב״ה sits in the heights of His world and distributes sustenance to every creature." The greatness of הקב״ה praised in this פרק is the granular care that ensures every בריה — every ant, fish, bird, human — receives its מזון. The proof-text is תהילים קל״ו:כ״ה: "נֹתֵן לֶחֶם לְכָל בָּשָׂר, כִּי לְעוֹלָם חַסְדּוֹ". This is the sugya that establishes הלל הגדול's status as a peak of daily/festival tefillah. ↩
  13. גמרא ברכות דף ד' — הלל הגדול almost daily instead of Ashrei — The גמרא במסכת ברכות דף ד' ע״ב discusses the daily obligation of אשרי יושבי ביתך (Ashrei — תהילים קמ״ה) — three times daily. The Gemara raises a passing hava amina: why not require הלל הגדול instead? Both share the alphabetic-acrostic feature Chazal valued; both praise הקב״ה broadly. The Gemara ultimately preserves Ashrei as the daily standard — but the fact that הלל הגדול was even considered speaks to its stature. We reserve it for weightier occasions: Shabbos morning, Pesach seder (in the Hallel portion). ↩
  14. הָאַדֶּרֶת וְהָאֱמוּנָה — from פרקי היכלות; מהרי״ל restriction — הָאַדֶּרֶת וְהָאֱמוּנָה לְחַי עוֹלָמִים — an alphabetical acrostic naming divine attributes across all letters, part of the ancient mystical literature of פרקי היכלות (the "Heichalos literature" describing the שירה of the מלאכים). The מהרי״ל (ר' יעקב מולין, ~1360–1427) ruled that this תפילה should be recited בציבור only on Yom Kippur — the one day when Klal Yisrael formally attains a status parallel to מלאכים (no eating/drinking, white bigadim, שבת שבתון). On other days, saying the שירה of the מלאכים risks being חוצפה. The חסידישע oylam nonetheless recites it every Shabbos, justifying it by the seventh-wing principle (see fn3 and last week's shiur): on Shabbos, Klal Yisrael is the seventh wing of the שרפים and therefore can say what the מלאכים would otherwise say. Chabad's ניגון is often attributed to Napoleon Bonaparte's march (specific composer disputed). ↩

  16. אבות דרבי נתן — Adam HaRishon composed it after the חטא — אבות דרבי נתן — one of the מסכתות קטנות appended to the Talmud, functioning as an extended tosefta on Pirkei Avos, generally attributed to R' Nathan HaBavli or a student, redacted through the Amoraic period. The relevant passage teaches: אדם הראשון committed the חטא of עץ הדעת late Friday afternoon. As Shabbos began and darkness fell — his first-ever experience of night — he assumed the sin had brought eternal darkness upon the world. He stayed awake, terrified. Shabbos morning, the sun rose. Overcome, he composed מזמור שיר ליום השבת. At the moment he began to sing, "באותה שעה ירדו כיתות של מלאכי השרת" — kittos of מלאכים descended with instruments (עלי עשור ועלי נבל, עלי הגיון בכנור).   17. ריטב״א על עבודה זרה — החטא מבלבלו — The ריטב״א (ר' יום טוב ן' אשבילי, c. 1250–1330) on מסכת עבודה זרה addresses the difficulty: Adam HaRishon was, per the Gemara's descriptions (see ב״ב דף נ״ח: "תפוח עקבו של אדם הראשון מכהה גלגל חמה"), a being of towering wisdom. Why would he not know that there is a natural cycle of ויהי ערב ויהי בוקר? The Ritva's answer: of course he knew — but "החטא מבלבלו" — sin disorients the mind. Even the greatest intellect, post-חטא, cannot access what it plainly knew before. The chiddush is theological as much as psychological: sin damages a person's ability to believe what he intellectually knows. ↩

  20. The Shimon Kefa legend — נשמת attributed to an undercover infiltrator — A legend/urban myth circulating in some Jewish sources — the Rav noted its currency at least back to the Rishonic era in Northern France (see fn21). The legend holds that Shimon Kefa (Peter — "Kefa" = "rock" in Aramaic, "Petros" in Greek) was a frum Yid appointed by the תנאים to infiltrate the early Christian movement. His mission: establish laws that would create a definitive separation between Christianity and Yiddishkeit. He is credited (per this legend) with instituting Sunday as the Christian day of rest — a single stroke that made the Shabbos-boundary between the two religions permanent. The legend then claims he composed נשמת and it was smuggled into our siddur. The historical Peter (Simon Peter, c. 1 – c. 65 CE) is a central figure in Christian tradition as the founder of the Roman Church; the "undercover Jewish agent" narrative is unique to Jewish popular literature and has no scholarly-historical basis. The legend is decisively rebutted by the מחזור ויטרי (fn21). ↩
    The Rav framed the sugya explicitly as "painful" and did not endorse the legend. His purpose in raising it was to introduce and endorse the מחזור ויטרי's rebuttal.
  21. מחזור ויטרי — the rebuttal, and the חטאת שמנה warning — The מחזור ויטרי — an important 11th-12th century Rishonic compilation, authored by ר' שמחה מויטרי, a talmid and חתן of Rashi. The Rav paraphrased (rather than quoted verbatim): anyone who claims נשמת was written by this Yid who became a Christian — even if undercover — will be חייב when the בית המקדש is rebuilt to bring a קרבן חטאת שמנה for uttering such a falsehood about a holy tefillah. The invocation of חטאת שמנה — a particularly weighty korban chatas category — measures how seriously the מחזור ויטרי treated the danger of the legend. His counter-attribution: נשמת was composed by the אַנְשֵׁי כְּנֶסֶת הַגְּדוֹלָה, the standard authorship for the fixed parts of our siddur. He brings multiple רָאיוֹת from the internal structure and vocabulary of the tefillah itself. ↩
    Standard reference: מחזור ויטרי, הלכות שבת / סדר תפילת שבת. Specific siman/os not cited in the shiur; the passage is well-known in the Rishonic literature on the origin of נשמת.
