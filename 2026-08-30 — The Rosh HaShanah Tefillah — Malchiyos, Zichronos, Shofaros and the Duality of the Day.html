Rabbi Ephrati · Sunday Morning שיעור on תפילה

The Rosh HaShanah Tefillah

מלכויות · זכרונות · שופרות — the core of the day, the historical development, and the duality of פחד and שמחה
Recorded 30 August 2026 · First of three shiurim on the מחזור before Rosh HaShanah (26 September 2026)

Post-recess, and with three weeks to ראש השנה, the Rav pivots from the standard series on תפילה to a focused arc on the מחזור. This first שיעור sets the frame: what is the core of the Rosh HaShanah davening (spoiler — not וּנְתַנֶּה תֹּקֶף, however moving it is), what did the משנה and גמרא actually require, how did רב's introductions and the גאונים's פיוטים get layered on top, and how a Yid holds simultaneously the פחד הדין and the שמחה של בטחון that make ראש השנה what it is. The opening story — a Nadvorna חסיד and his Rebbe on the eve of departure — sets the tone: the סידור is in its place, the מחזור is arranged — אתה צריך לְסַדֵּר את עצמך.

I. Approaching the Yamim Nora'im — a Study in Contradiction

We approach the ימים נוראים with a demand few other times of the year impose: two seemingly opposite emotions held simultaneously. פחד הדין — the terror of judgment. And בטחון — the confidence that we, as part of the כלל, will be inscribed favorably. To hold these together is a genuine avodah; the tefillah and structure of ראש השנה is built to walk us through it.

II. First Preparation — סַדֵּר אֶת עַצְמְךָ

A Story from the Nadvorna Chassidishe Court

A חסיד of the Multshleif Nadvorna Rebbe[1] came to inform his Rebbe that he was leaving to serve as a שליח ציבור for ראש השנה and יום כיפור. "Why leave now," asked the Rebbe, "right before ראש השנה?" The חסיד answered: "I need to be מְסַדֵּר the מחזור, to prepare the תפילות."

The Rebbe's reply: "הַסִּדּוּר בִּמְקוֹמוֹ, וְהַמַּחְזוֹר מְסֻדָּר. אַתָּה צָרִיךְ לְסַדֵּר אֶת עַצְמְךָ." — The prayer book is in its place. The מחזור is already arranged. You need to arrange yourself.

The first preparation for ראש השנה is not the מחזור — it is you. The מחזור will be learned, but the core is internal preparation: יישוב הדעת, בירור הכוונה, alignment of one's נפש to the day. Even as this arc explores the מחזור, the primacy is on the person's inner journey — preparing yourself for מלכויות, זכרונות, ושופרות.

III. The Core of the Day — Beyond the Piyutim

A serious talmid of this שיעור knows the fundamental approach: understand where everything comes from and why. The סידור — and the מחזור even more so — expanded over the generations. This expansion may reflect a divine will: that we say more, daven more, feel more. In earlier times, perhaps only minimal words were needed to access the קדושה and the ideas of the day. Today the מחזור is long, and we must ask: what is the core?

Ask any Yid which part of ראש השנה or יום כיפור davening moves them most and the answer is almost universally: וּנְתַנֶּה תֹּקֶף. And rightfully so. In ונתנה תוקף, we encounter the most dramatic, vivid description of what transpires in שמים on these days — מלאכים running and trembling, the great שופר blast followed by קול דממה דקה, all trembling before the יום הדין, then the climax: תשובה, תפילה, וצדקה overturn the decree.

But ונתנה תוקף is a much later פיוט.[2] Its authorship and dating are the subject of significant historical dispute. What is certain: אביי and רבא never said ונתנה תוקף. The real תפילה of ראש השנה, as described in the גמרא, consists of פסוקים — nothing more.

Ironically, what do שלוחי ציבור and קהילות often race through most quickly? The פסוקים — the very core. We give the piyutim our attention because we are human beings; drama moves us, a bare pasuk less so. But looking at חז״ל in the משנה and גמרא, the core of ראש השנה davening is: מלכויות, זכרונות, שופרות.

IV. The Unique Structure of Musaf — Nine ברכות, Not Seven

The תורה's Names for the Day

The תורה gives ראש השנה four names: יום תרועה, זכרון תרועה, מקרא קודש, ושבתון. Notably, the תורה never uses the phrase יום הזיכרון. Our formulation — מְקַדֵּשׁ יוֹם הַזִּכָּרוֹן — comes from חז״ל, not from the pasuk. The Torah tells us little about specific prayers; it tells us only that this is a יום טוב, that a שופר must be sounded.

The Chiddush of Chazal in Rosh HaShanah

It was חז״ל in the משנה ראש השנה[3] who established a unique structure. Every יום טוב's מוסף consists of seven ברכות: the first three, the last three, and one middle ברכה for the קדושת היום. On ראש השנה, however, חז״ל added three additional ברכות: מלכויות (proclaiming God's sovereignty), זכרונות (invoking His remembrance), and שופרות (centered on the shofar). Ten ברכות in principle — but the גמרא[4] rules that מלכויות is combined with the middle ברכה of קדושת היום, yielding nine ברכות — a structure unique in the entire year.

Why the combination? The גמרא teaches: "כְּדֵי שֶׁתַּמְלִיכוּנִי עֲלֵיכֶם" — so that you crown Me over you. The קדושת היום of ראש השנה is מלכות. The two are not separate; they are one act.

V. Historical Development — Tannaim, Rav, and the Piyyutim

The Machloikes of the Tannaim on the Placement of Malchiyos

The precise placement of מלכויות was itself the subject of a major machloikes among the תנאים.[5] רבי עקיבא held — as is our practice today — that מלכויות should be incorporated into the middle unique ברכה of the מוסף עמידה. רבי יוחנן בן נוּרִי argued that מלכויות should instead be integrated into the third ברכה of קדושת השם, recited daily as part of אתה קדוש, culminating in הַמֶּלֶךְ הַקָּדוֹשׁ.

Our הלכה is not decided according to רבי יוחנן בן נורי, but in the era of the משנה the practice was not uniform. The ירושלמי[6] relates that in the גליל they followed רבי יוחנן בן נורי; in יהודה they followed רבי עקיבא. The uniformity we take for granted took centuries to consolidate.

The Content in the Time of the Tannaim — Ten Pesukim per Section

In the time of the תנאים, the content of these special ברכות was concise. For each section — מלכויות, זכרונות, שופרות — they recited ten פסוקים. The גמרא[7] specifies the composition: three from the תורה, three from כתובים (specifically תהילים), three from נביאים, and a concluding tenth pasuk from the תורה. That was the entirety. No introductions, no piyutim. Just the pesukim, three sets of ten, framed by the ברכות.

Tekah D'Rav — the Amoraic Introductions

Then came רב, one of the greatest of the אמוראים, who established what is known as תָּקַע דְּרַב — "Rav's establishment."[8] He composed thematic introductions to be recited before the פסוקים of each section:

  • עָלֵינוּ לְשַׁבֵּחַ for מלכויות
  • אַתָּה זוֹכֵר מַעֲשֵׂה עוֹלָם for זכרונות
  • אַתָּה נִגְלֵיתָ בַּעֲנַן כְּבוֹדֶךָ for שופרות

Rav's introductions became fixed liturgy. עלינו, in particular, was later extracted from its ראש השנה home and made the daily closing of every תפילה — but its original context is here, opening the מלכויות of ראש השנה.

The Piyutim — Geonim and Rishonim

The פיוטים were added much later, in the era of the גאונים and the early ראשונים. Great liturgical poets — the פייטנים — took the central themes and wove them into intricate poetic form. This creates a modern challenge: for those who do not understand לשון הקודש well, reciting these complex פיוטים without comprehension may miss the point.

A Practical Suggestion Rather than rushing through all the פיוטים, focus on one. Study its meaning in advance. Say that one פיוט with true כוונה. Your תפילה will be far more elevated than if you race through the full catalog with comprehension of none.

VI. The Three Core Themes

מלכויות — Coronating the King

The גמרא states the reason for מלכויות directly: "כְּדֵי שֶׁתַּמְלִיכוּנִי עֲלֵיכֶם" — so that you crown Me as King over you.

Rav Soloveitchik's Chaslavich Story — Kroining Nacht

הגאון הרב יוסף דב סולובייצ'יק זצ״ל[9] recounted a story from his childhood in the חסידישע shtetl of Chaslavich. One ראש השנה, his first-grade רבי — a לוּבַּאבִיטשער חסיד — walked into class with his face glowing. He asked the children: "Do you know what tonight is? Tonight is Kroining Nacht — Coronation Night. Tonight we inaugurate the coronation of הקדוש ברוך הוא. We place a crown on His head, כביכול. And do you know who places the crown? Yankl the tailor, Berl the shoemaker — every simple Yid gets to ascend to the כסא הכבוד and crown הקב״ה. We declare: ריבונו של עולם — You are our מלך."

That is the essence of מלכויות. The tailor's crown and the shoemaker's crown together become the crown of the King.

זכרונות — The Dimension of יום הדין

The second theme is זכרונות, reflecting the dimension of יום הדין. The גמרא explains the reason: "כְּדֵי שֶׁיַּעֲלֶה זִכְרוֹנְכֶם לְפָנַי לְטוֹבָה" — so that your remembrance rises before Me for the good. In the ברכה of זכרונות we remind הקב״ה — כביכול — that He remembers everything, most centrally עֲקֵדַת יִצְחָק, and therefore should grant us a complete גאולה.

שופרות — The Voice of History

The third theme is שופרות — the עבודה of the day. We blow the שופר and we speak about the שופר. The first great שופר blast in history was at מתן תורה. The ultimate שופר will sound at the final redemption: תְּקִיעָה גְּדוֹלָה לְחֵרוּתֵנוּ. Between the first שופר and the last שופר sits every שופר blast of Jewish history — including the ones we sound tomorrow morning. All of this is woven into the ברכה of שופרות.

VII. The Duality — פחד and שמחה

The Dimension of פחד

On one hand, this is a day of פחד and אימה. The גמרא במסכת ראש השנה דף ט״ז ע״ב[10] teaches that three ספרים are opened: one for the צדיקים, one for the רשעים, and one for the בינוניים — where most of us are — who are תְּלוּיִים וְעוֹמְדִים, suspended. We hang between life and death for ten days, until יום כיפור.

As רבי חיים שמואל לוויט[11] put it: it is as if the noose is around our necks, and we have ten days for our תשובה and מעשים to come forward as witnesses to save us. This is why real פחד belongs on ראש השנה.

The Dimension of שמחה and בטחון

On the other hand, we wear בגדי לבן, dress in יום טוב clothing, and eat well. The גמרא asks why we do not say הלל on ראש השנה — and answers that the books of life and death are open. How can we sing songs of praise?[12] And yet the same גמרא records the other side: לוֹבְשִׁין לְבָנִין וּמִתְעַטְּפִין לְבָנִין — we dress in white and come with שמחה.

The pasuk in נחמיה ח':י[13] is explicit:

"לְכוּ אִכְלוּ מַשְׁמַנִּים וּשְׁתוּ מַמְתַקִּים... כִּי חֶדְוַת ה' הִיא מָעֻזְּכֶם."
Go, eat rich foods and drink sweet drinks... for the joy of ה' is your strength.

There was even a מנהג, recorded by the פרי חדש[14], to send משלוח מנות on ראש השנה, just as on פורים. The joy comes from בטחון — we trust that הקב״ה, our Father and our Judge, will make an עת for us.

The Synthesis — גילו ברעדה

Only a Yid can do this. Only we can take two days and simultaneously hold both the פחד and אימה of the דין and the שמחה and בטחון that He is our Father. This is the meaning of the pasuk in תהילים ב':י״א[15]:

"עִבְדוּ אֶת ה' בְּיִרְאָה וְגִילוּ בִּרְעָדָה."
Serve ה' with fear, and rejoice with trembling.

The trembling and the joy exist together. Not sequentially — not "first the פחד, then the שמחה." Simultaneously. That is ראש השנה.

VIII. Personal כוונה and Communal Identity

A common error: rushing שמונה עשרה to "keep pace" with the tzibbur. On the contrary — your personal עמידה can and should be longer if you are taking time to speak to הקב״ה. The פיוטים are the sidebar; the עיקר is the שמונה עשרה itself and the פסוקים within it.

But that personal focus must also be balanced against the truth that we daven ראש השנה as a ציבור. The זוהר[16] applies the pasuk "בְּתוֹךְ עַמִּי אָנֹכִי יֹשָׁבֶת" (מלכים ב' ד':י״ג) to the day: on ראש השנה, one must be among my people. Our prayers are in the plural — חָטָאנוּ לְפָנֶיךָ. When you daven on ראש השנה, you are part of a beautiful community; within the embrace of the ציבור, an individual does not get singled out. By being part of the whole, we are saved.

As the משנה in אבות א':י״ד[17] teaches: "אִם אֵין אֲנִי לִי, מִי לִי? וּכְשֶׁאֲנִי לְעַצְמִי, מָה אֲנִי?" — If I am not for myself, who will be for me? But when I am for myself alone, what am I? Both halves apply to ראש השנה simultaneously.

IX. The Weight of the Day — גזר דין

To grasp the gravity of ראש השנה, consider a letter from הרב אליהו דסלר in מכתב מאליהו.[18] He wrote to his son: all the צרות, יסורים, and עגמת נפש of the past year were decreed on the previous ראש השנה. If we had only prayed with more כוונה, the decree could have been different. This day has the כח to change the גזר דין.

Reb Elimelech and Reb Zusha — the Decree Cannot Be Switched

A famous story about Reb Elimelech of Lizhensk and his brother Reb Zusha of Anipoli[19] illustrates the point. While wandering in self-imposed גלות, they were staying in a tavern. Some drunken peasants decided to beat Reb Zusha three times. Reb Elimelech, feeling it was unfair — his brother should not bear this — switched beds with Reb Zusha, hoping the peasants would beat him instead. But when the peasants came back, intending now to beat the other man, they ended up grabbing Reb Zusha again — three times.

Reb Zusha later explained: "On ראש השנה, it was decreed that I should be beaten three times. You can switch beds all you want, but if הקב״ה decrees something, it will happen."

That is the essence of the day. The גזר דין is decreed. If you want to prevent the hardships of the coming year, this is the day to do it. The only way is through focused עבודה — proper תפילה, sincere תשובה, and hearing the שופר. When you recite מלכויות, זכרונות, ושופרות, hold both the awe and the trust. With that posture, you may merit a good judgment and be נִכְתָּב וְנֶחְתָּם בְּסֵפֶר צַדִּיקִים לְחַיִּים טוֹבִים.

X. Practical Takeaways

  • Prepare yourself first, not the מחזור. The מחזור is already arranged. You are the variable.
  • The core of ראש השנה davening is מלכויות, זכרונות, שופרות. The פיוטים are the layer above; the פסוקים inside the ברכות are the עיקר.
  • Don't rush the פסוקים. They are precisely what חז״ל required.
  • Pick one פיוט whose meaning you understand, and say it with כוונה. Better one פיוט with understanding than the full catalog raced.
  • Hold both פחד and שמחה. The white bigadim, the sweet foods, the mishloach manos per the פרי חדש — these are the שמחה side. The תלויים ועומדים is the פחד side. גילו ברעדה.
  • Personal כוונה is not against the ציבור. Your עמידה can be longer. But the בְּתוֹךְ עַמִּי matters too. The Yid is saved as part of Klal Yisrael.

The formula "כדי שתמליכוני עליכם" is the Gemara's answer to why מלכויות is embedded specifically in the קדושת היום ברכה: the קדושת היום of ראש השנה is the coronation of הקב״ה. Parallel formulas for the other two sections: זכרונות — "כדי שיעלה זכרונכם לפני לטובה"; שופרות — the עבודה and זכר לשופר של מתן תורה ולעתיד לבוא. ↩

We pasken like רבי עקיבא. ↩

    The Rav cited "the Gemara" for this — the specific reference is Yerushalmi Rosh HaShanah, halacha 6 in perek 4 (per the Vilna edition foliation).
  7. גמרא ראש השנה ל״ב — the ten pesukim per section (3 Torah + 3 Ketuvim + 3 Neviim + 1 Torah) — The גמרא ראש השנה דף ל״ב ע״א specifies the required composition of pesukim within each of the three special sections. Each of מלכויות, זכרונות, and שופרות requires ten פסוקים, broken down as: three from the תורה, three from כתובים (specifically תהילים), three from נביאים, and a concluding tenth pasuk drawn again from the תורה. This gives the sections a distinctive rhythmic structure — Torah → Ketuvim → Neviim → Torah as a closing anchor. In the era of the תנאים, this was the entirety of the ברכה's substance beyond the opening/closing formulas. ↩
  8. תָּקַע דְּרַב — Rav's introductions: עלינו לשבח, אתה זוכר, אתה נגלית — רב (Abba Arikha, first-generation Amora, c. 175–247 CE) composed thematic introductions to each of the three special sections of מוסף. The term "תָּקַע דְּרַב" (literally "Rav's insertion" or "Rav's establishment") refers to this liturgical addition. The three introductions are: עָלֵינוּ לְשַׁבֵּחַ for מלכויות; אַתָּה זוֹכֵר מַעֲשֵׂה עוֹלָם for זכרונות; אַתָּה נִגְלֵיתָ בַּעֲנַן כְּבוֹדֶךָ for שופרות. Note that עלינו was originally composed for ראש השנה מלכויות and only later extracted and appended as the closing of every תפילה — Rav is the source both for its ראש השנה role and (indirectly) for its ubiquity in daily davening today. ↩
    The attribution of עלינו specifically to רב is standard among the Rishonim (Machzor Vitry, Kol Bo, etc.) but scholarly opinion also finds evidence of the piyut in pre-Amoraic Hekhalos literature — the Rav's authorship of the מוסף version is compatible with an earlier Hekhalos-tradition ancestor.
  9. הגר״י דב סולובייצ׳יק — the Chaslavich Kroining Nacht story — הגאון הרב יוסף דב סולובייצ׳יק זצ״ל (the "Rav" of Boston, 1903–1993). The Chaslavich story — his first-grade Lubavitcher רבי calling ראש השנה night "Kroining Nacht" and describing every simple Yid as ascending the כסא הכבוד to crown הקב״ה — appears in several published collections of Rav Soloveitchik's ראש השנה derashos, notably in Days of Deliverance and Before Hashem You Shall Be Purified. It is one of the Rav's signature stories on the theme of the personal-and-universal coronation. ↩
  10. גמרא ראש השנה ט״ז ע״ב — three ספרים; תלויים ועומדים — The גמרא במסכת ראש השנה דף ט״ז ע״ב, quoting רבי כרוספדאי אמר רבי יוחנן: on ראש השנה three ספרים are opened before הקב״ה — one for the רשעים גמורים, one for the צדיקים גמורים, and one for the בינוניים. The צדיקים are inscribed and sealed immediately for life; the רשעים for death; the בינוניים are "תְּלוּיִים וְעוֹמְדִים" — suspended from ראש השנה until יום כיפור. The ten days of תשובה are given specifically to those in the middle group to shift their standing. ↩
  11. רבי חיים שמואל לוויט — the noose-around-neck framing — The specific mashal — that the בינוני has a "noose around his neck" during the ten days, and his תשובה and מעשים must come forward as witnesses to save him — was cited by the Rav in the name of רבי חיים שמואל לוויט. Standard reference for the ten-day suspension theology: מסילת ישרים, שערי תשובה, and the various derashos of רבי חיים שמואל לוויט preserved in his talmidim's compilations. ↩

  12. Gemara — no Hallel on ראש השנה because the books of life and death are open — The גמרא במסכת ראש השנה דף ל״ב ע״ב discusses why we do not say הלל on ראש השנה and יום כיפור despite their being major מועדים. The answer: "אפשר מלך יושב על כסא דין וספרי חיים וספרי מתים פתוחין לפניו וישראל אומרים שירה?" — how can Klal Yisrael sing songs of praise while the King sits on the throne of judgment and the books of life and death are open? The absence of הלל is itself a form of פחד. And yet — the parallel practice of לובשין לבנין and חדות ה' היא מעוזכם co-exists. ↩
  13. נחמיה ח':י — חדות ה' היא מעוזכם — Full pasuk from נחמיה פרק ח' פסוק י': "וַיֹּאמֶר לָהֶם: לְכוּ אִכְלוּ מַשְׁמַנִּים וּשְׁתוּ מַמְתַקִּים, וְשִׁלְחוּ מָנוֹת לְאֵין נָכוֹן לוֹ — כִּי־קָדוֹשׁ הַיּוֹם לַאֲדֹנֵינוּ; וְאַל־תֵּעָצֵבוּ, כִּי־חֶדְוַת ה' הִיא מָעֻזְּכֶם." The context is עזרא הסופר reading the תורה to the returned Babylonian exiles on ראש השנה — the assembly wept when they heard the words of the Torah, and Ezra told them: do not weep; this is a holy day; go eat rich foods, drink sweet drinks, and send portions (מנות) to those who have none; the joy of ה' is your strength. This pasuk is the halachic-textual anchor for the שמחה side of ראש השנה. ↩
  14. פרי חדש — מנהג of משלוח מנות on ראש השנה — The פרי חדש (רבי חזקיה די סילוה, 1659–1698), commentary on שולחן ערוך אורח חיים, records the מנהג of sending משלוח מנות on ראש השנה, drawing on the נחמיה ח':י pasuk (fn13, שלחו מנות לאין נכון לו). The practice has fallen out of universal observance — most contemporary קהילות associate משלוח מנות exclusively with פורים — but the פרי חדש preserves it as a legitimate ancient practice for ראש השנה, rooted in the נחמיה context. ↩
    Specific siman in Pri Chadash Orach Chaim was not cited in the shiur — likely siman תקצ"ז (the ראש השנה siman) or the general siman on הלכות ראש השנה.
  15. תהילים ב':י״א — עבדו את ה' ביראה וגילו ברעדה — Full pasuk from תהילים פרק ב' פסוק י״א: "עִבְדוּ אֶת ה' בְּיִרְאָה, וְגִילוּ בִּרְעָדָה." — "Serve ה' with fear, and rejoice with trembling." The גמרא במסכת ברכות דף ל' ע״ב uses this pasuk to establish the halachic principle that intense שמחה and intense יראה must coexist in עבודת ה'. The pasuk is the classical source for the theological synthesis of ראש השנה's duality — the joy is not despite the trembling but within it. ↩
  16. זוהר — בתוך עמי אנכי יושבת applied to ראש השנה — The זוהר חלק ג' דף ק״א ע״א (פרשת אמור) applies the pasuk from מלכים ב' פרק ד' פסוק י״ג — "בְּתוֹךְ עַמִּי אָנֹכִי יֹשָׁבֶת", the Shunamite woman's response to Elisha when asked what she needs from the king — to the theology of communal prayer on ראש השנה. The individual who says I dwell among my people — that is, who does not seek to be singled out for special treatment — is granted the communal protection: when the accuser searches for individuals to indict, the person embedded in the ציבור is not visible as a target. This זוהר is the classical source for the halachic-mystical priority on davening ב"ציבור on the ימים נוראים. ↩
  17. אבות א':י״ד — Hillel: אם אין אני לי מי לי — מסכת אבות פרק א' משנה י״ד — "הוּא הָיָה אוֹמֵר: אִם אֵין אֲנִי לִי, מִי לִי? וּכְשֶׁאֲנִי לְעַצְמִי, מָה אֲנִי? וְאִם לֹא עַכְשָׁיו, אֵימָתָי?" — the famous three-line teaching of הלל הזקן. The Rav invoked it here for the balance between personal responsibility (אם אין אני לי מי לי) and communal identity (וכשאני לעצמי מה אני). Both are essential on ראש השנה; neither alone suffices. ↩
  18. רב אליהו דסלר — מכתב מאליהו: prior ראש השנה's tefillah decrees the year's tribulations — מכתב מאליהו, by רבי אליהו אליעזר דסלר זצ״ל (1892–1953), Bnei Brak. In a letter to his son (published in later volumes of the collection), Rav Dessler argues that all the צרות, יסורים, and עגמת נפש a person experiences in a given year were decreed on the previous ראש השנה. Had the person's tefillah on that ראש השנה been more focused, the year's decree could have been substantially different. The letter's point is not fatalism but the opposite: the day of ראש השנה carries real causal weight on the coming year, and the tefillah on it deserves corresponding investment. ↩

  19. Reb Elimelech of Lizhensk and Reb Zusha of Anipoli — the tavern beating story — A famous chassidishe story about the two brothers רבי אלימלך מליזענסק (1717–1787, author of נועם אלימלך) and רבי זושא מאניפולי (c. 1718–1800, brother and close chaver of Reb Elimelech). Both were talmidim of the מגיד ממעזריטש. The two spent years in self-imposed גלות, wandering from town to town in disguise, doing תשובה for Klal Yisrael. The tavern-and-beating story circulates in multiple versions in chassidishe biographical anthologies (Sipurei Chassidim, Ma'ase HaGedolim, etc.); its point is the immovability of a גזר דין once decreed on ראש השנה. The specific version cited in the שיעור closes with Reb Zusha's line: "You can switch beds all you want, but if הקב״ה decrees something, it will happen." ↩
