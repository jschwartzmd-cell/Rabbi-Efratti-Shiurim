Rabbi Ephrati · Sunday Morning שיעור on תפילה

Customs and Tefillot of ליל שבת

אנא בכח, כגוונא, ובמה מדליקין — Their Origins, Disputes, and Spiritual Logic
Recorded 1 March 2026 · Reworked from the original שיעור

After tracing the structure of קבלת שבת last week, the שיעור turned this Sunday to the texts that bracket it on either side — אנא בכח within the procession itself, and כגוונא and במה מדליקין on its threshold. Each of the three carries its own history: אנא בכח is an ancient kabbalistic prayer, perhaps composed by נחוניא בן הקנה; כגוונא was introduced by the בעל שם טוב and his תלמידים; and במה מדליקין, after a long and shifting history, became a quiet battlefield between חסידים and מתנגדים. Where each prayer sits in our siddur is, in part, the record of those disputes.

I. אנא בכח — The Forty-Two Letter Name

Between מזמור לדוד and לכה דודי we say אנא בכח. The text is brief — seven lines of six words each, forty-two words in all. The structure is not poetic ornament; it is built around the שם בן מ״ב — the forty-two-letter Name[1]. The initial letters of the forty-two words are the שם itself, which is why, after reciting it (quietly), we add ברוך שם כבוד מלכותו לעולם ועד: one has just, perhaps unknowingly, uttered the Name.

Authorship is traditionally attributed to רבי נחוניא בן הקנה[2], the תנא from whose בית מדרש the early היכלות literature emerges. Whether the attribution is historically airtight or, as the academic literature of יהדות holds, the prayer surfaces somewhat later in early kabbalistic circles, the centrality of the name itself goes back to חז״ל. The גמרא בקידושין דף ע״א ע״א[3] states the conditions under which the שם בן מ״ב may be transmitted. The link to creation, as the שיעור presented it, runs through the משנה in חגיגה י״א ב, אין דורשין במעשה בראשית בשנים: the תוספות there (as the Rav reported them) explain that מעשה בראשית is the שם בן מ״ב, by which the world was created. The ערוך השולחן therefore suggests that because קבלת שבת welcomes the Shabbos of creation, אנא בכח is placed here. That is the ערוך השולחן's suggestion, and no other source for the placement was found.

Why This Placement? The shiur gave one explanation for the placement between מזמור לדוד and לכה דודי, the suggestion of the ערוך השולחן: since מעשה בראשית is bound up with the שם בן מ״ב, and קבלת שבת welcomes the Shabbos that crowns creation, אנא בכח belongs here. The Rav searched for other sources and found none. (Authorship and the Arizal's role in the placement were not discussed; לכה דודי itself dates only from the Ari's time.)

II. Standing and Bowing at לכה דודי

Standing

The mainstream minhag — recorded in the ערוך השולחן, אורח חיים סימן רס״ב[4] — is to stand only for the final stanza, בואי בשלום, and to turn toward the entrance (in the shiur's words, toward the west of the בית מדרש), re-enacting what the אמוראים did when they walked out to greet the שבת. There is, however, a stricter custom — found among חב״ד, and, it was suggested in the room, among some Syrian communities — to stand for the entirety of לכה דודי, a sign of the worshiper's excitement that שבת is entering.

Bowing — Left then Right, or Right then Left?

Two minhagim exist. Some bow first to the left, then to the right, then to the center; others reverse the order. The reasoning given for the "left first" custom is that the worshiper's left is the שכינה's right; the Rav was candid that he did not know what that means, and his own practice is left, right, center. The שיעור's pragmatic ruling: both customs have a source and both are valid; what matters is the הרגש — the inner sense — that one is greeting the שבת. The act is choreography, not arithmetic.

III. ליל שבת That Is Also יום טוב

When ערב שבת falls on יום טוב, many minhagim omit לכה דודי entirely or in part, and adjust the structure of קבלת שבת. Practice varies by community: some omit לכה דודי entirely, some say only its first and last stanzas, and some Ashkenazim do not say it at all. The טור (אורח חיים סימן ר״ע)[5] records that in Ashkenaz במה מדליקין is likewise not said on a יום טוב that falls on ערב שבת. Two reasons were offered for the omission of לכה דודי, and the Rav found neither fully satisfying:

The kavod-of-יום-טוב reason: A separate elaborate procession for שבת in the middle of יום טוב would suggest that יום טוב is somehow inadequate, requiring an additional welcoming ceremony. Out of כבוד יום טוב, the special שבת greeting is muted.

The kedushah-already-present reason: A worshiper on יום טוב is already in a state of קדושה. The transition into שבת is not from חול to קודש but from קודש to greater קודש. The dramatic procession that suits the weekday-to-Shabbos transition does not fit a kedushah-to-kedushah one.

The Rav was not persuaded by either. Of the first he said plainly that he did not understand why accepting שבת should shame יום טוב. Against the second he pressed that the קדושה of שבת is higher than that of יום טוב: on שבת even אוכל נפש-מלאכות (cooking and similar) are forbidden, and there is a נשמה יתירה that יום טוב does not have. A participant offered אין מערבין שמחה בשמחה. The Rav concluded that communities differ in exactly which parts they say, and left the matter with יש להאריך בזה.

IV. במה מדליקין — Three Reasons, One Mishnah

After קבלת שבת and מעריב, our minhag is to recite the second perek of מסכת שבת — the famous במה מדליקין[6]. The perek itself is straightforward halachah: which oils and wicks may be used for נר שבת, and which may not — a concern because one might tilt the lamp to brighten the flame, which is a desecration of שבת. The closing mishnah lists שלושה דברים צריך אדם לומר בתוך ביתו ערב שבת עם חשכה: עישרתם? עירבתם? הדליקו את הנר. So why this perek of all perakim, and why here, on Friday night?

Reason 1 — The Latecomers

This reason, which the Rav gave in the name of רב נחשון גאון[7] (and the Siddur of רב עמרם גאון records the custom itself), is the most touching. In the era of the Geonim, shuls were typically located outside the city walls — in the fields, away from population centers. A late-arriver on Friday night, finishing his עמידה after the rest of the קהל had already left, would face a long walk home in the dark, alone, without a torch (which he could not carry on שבת), exposed to מזיקין — the ambient dangers of nighttime travel.

חז״ל and the Geonim, anxious to protect that one late-arriver, instituted a five-to-seven minute reading, said after קדיש at the end of davening, that the whole קהל would say together — keeping everyone in the shul long enough for the late-comer to finish his תפילה and walk home together with the others. במה מדליקין, by length and content, was the right size for the job.

A Pattern in Halachah The שיעור pointed out that this is one of many examples — not the only one — of חז״ל restructuring the davening to protect latecomers. The doubled ברכה structure of עליות (one before, one after, rather than one for the first and one for the last עולה only), the two-bracket structure of certain ברכות, and several other features of our nusach are recorded by the Geonim as accommodations for those who arrive late or are still davening. The texture of our siddur is, in this sense, profoundly merciful.

Reason 2 — A Reminder of שבת Preparations

The second reason, which the Rav attributed to the מחזור ויטרי and other Rishonim[8], is mnemonic; the טור likewise notes that the perek contains the law of kindling and the three things a person must say at home. The closing mishnah reminds each person of what must be done by nightfall: have the produce been tithed (עישרתם), has an עירוב חצירות been made so that one may carry (עירבתם), and have the candles been lit (הדליקו את הנר).

The אבודרהם (whom the Rav called יוסף כהן אבודרהם; the author is ר' דוד אבודרהם) argued that, given this reminder function, it should be said before מעריב — so that a person who realizes he has forgotten something can still go home and fix it before שבת actually enters. This was, by his testimony, the original minhag. The shift to saying it after מעריב — which is now nearly universal Ashkenaz practice — happened later, on the reasoning that by the time we daven מעריב today, שבת is already in; nothing can be "fixed" anymore. The recitation becomes more a lesson for next week than a corrective for this week.

Reason 3 — The Polemic Against the Karaites

The third reason, which the Rav gave in the name of "some Rishonim"[9], is historical-polemical. In the eighth century, in Babylon, a man named ענן בן דוד[10] emerged from the household of the ראש גלות, a descendant of the family of בוסתנאי and of the House of David. The Rav told it this way: the sages of the generation refused him the office, he informed against them to the authorities in Baghdad and landed in jail himself, was released after some months, and then turned against the rabbinic authorities of his day and aligned himself with what would become the Karaite movement — a sect that rejected the תורה שבעל פה and read the תורה שבכתב in a strict literalist manner. (Historians generally say that Anan was passed over and his brother appointed; the Rav's version, that Anan did hold the office, is his own.)

One of the most visible Karaite practices was their reading of לא תבערו אש בכל מושבותיכם (Shemos 35:3) as forbidding any fire to be burning in a Jewish home on שבת — even one that had been lit before שבת entered. The Karaites therefore sat in cold, dark houses on Friday night.

In response, some Rishonim hold, the Geonim (the Rav named רב נטרונאי גאון, רב האי and רב עמרם) instituted — through the public, congregational recitation of the second perek of מסכת שבת, the very perek that codifies which fires may be lit before שבת and continue to burn — the legitimacy of the Oral Law's reading. To recite במה מדליקין in shul on Friday night was to make a public, communal declaration: we are חכמי המסורה; we read the text through the lens of חז״ל; our שבת is illuminated, our food is warm, and our homes are alive precisely because of the תורה שבעל פה.

V. כגוונא — The Baal Shem Tov's Insertion

Between קבלת שבת and מעריב, חסידים say כגוונא דאינון מתייחדין — a passage in Aramaic from the זוהר, פרשת תרומה[11]. As the Rav described it, the passage praises and elevates the stature of שבת: on שבת the harsh judgments (כל שולטני רוגזין) do not hold sway.

The text is from the זוהר, but the זוהר itself does not say to recite it on Friday night. The practice of inserting it into the formal davening is a takanah of the בעל שם טוב הקדוש and his תלמידים[12], as part of the broader Chassidic project of integrating zoharic texts into communal liturgy. In most siddurim the opening of כגוונא is printed in bold and the final short paragraph in small type. The Rav stressed that he knew of no source for saying it before the בעל שם טוב.

The Piaseczner Rebbe

The שיעור highlighted the writings of the פיאסעצנער רבי — ר' קלונימוס קלמיש שפירא (1889–1943)[13], a descendant of the נועם אלימלך. The Piaseczner was murdered in the Holocaust in 1943; his son, daughter-in-law and sister-in-law were killed earlier, in the bombing of Warsaw in 1939. His manuscripts buried in milk cans in the Warsaw Ghetto were recovered after the war and published as אש קודש. He founded a yeshiva in Warsaw, was a rising star among the young Chassidic rebbes and famous as a mashpiya, and is increasingly read today across denominational lines.

The Rav cited the Piaseczner's חובת התלמידים for כגוונא: a Jew who says it with kavanah, with joy and with love, taking upon himself the קדושה of שבת, gives the הקדוש ברוך הוא great נחת רוח in heaven. (The Rav's aside, "how does he know? I don't know," is part of the report.)

The Closing Paragraph and ברכו

A small textual concern: in most siddurim the opening of כגוונא is in bold, and a final short paragraph is in small type, with a note (even in the Artscroll siddur) that it is for one who davens alone. The paragraph ends by saying ולימא ברכו את ה' המברך. Ordinarily we do not say ברכו without a מנין of ten to answer. The question: may a person davening alone say it?

The answer brought in the שיעור: one who davens alone is not saying ברכו as a call to prayer that requires ten; he is reading a passage of the זוהר, which itself says ולימא ברכו. The Rav added that most people, even when davening alone on Friday night, do not say this paragraph, and that the leader (the בעל תפילה) is the one who says it.

VI. חסידים and מתנגדים — A Liturgical Front

The placement of במה מדליקין in modern Ashkenaz davening turns out to be a small monument to the great dispute of the late 18th century between חסידים and מתנגדים. To trace the dispute through this single liturgical decision:

The original placement: after מעריב and קדיש, at the very end of davening. This is the placement of רב עמרם גאון and רב נחשון גאון, the oldest attested, and the Rav said that in the times of the Rishonim, in Spain, Germany and France, it was said at the end of davening. Later, the Spanish-Portuguese communities (Amsterdam, London, Spain, Portugal) said it after לכה דודי and before מזמור שיר ליום השבת, and the שולחן ערוך (או״ח ר״ע) records that the Sefaradim say it before מעריב.

The Chassidic placement: חסידים inserted כגוונא after קבלת שבת and before מעריב — which, the Rav said, the מתנגדים could not stand: the חסידים, instead of relying primarily on הלכה, followed קבלה, and in a dispute between the בעלי ההלכה and the מקובלים we rule according to הלכה. (The Rav himself noted that a line is drawn between the Ari and the בית יוסף.) To the מתנגדים, this was a reversal of the proper hierarchy: halachah determines the structure of davening, not kabbalah.

The Misnagdic placement: according to the Rav, the first source to place במה מדליקין between קבלת שבת and מעריב, after קדיש יתום and before ברכו, is ר' אברהם דאנציג, the חיי אדם[14], a close follower of the Vilna Gaon. The Rav presented the motive as a theory ("this is the theory"), not a documented fact. The polemical logic, on that theory, is sharp: where the חסידים place a kabbalistic text (כגוונא) at the seam between קבלת שבת and מעריב, the מתנגדים place a halachic text (במה מדליקין, the Mishnah's own halachah of שבת) at that same seam. The placement says: halachah comes first; kabbalah is subordinate.

Ashkenaz siddurim today print במה מדליקין exactly there — after קדיש יתום and before ברכו — and the Rav, on this theory, saw the placement as the inheritance of the מתנגדים' liturgical protest.

A Living Map The next time you open a siddur on Friday night, the geography of where each prayer sits — אנא בכח between מזמור לדוד and לכה דודי, then either כגוונא (in the Chassidic and Sefard rite) or במה מדליקין (in Ashkenaz) between קבלת שבת and ברכו — is not arbitrary. On the Rav's account it is the legible record of the Geonim's concern for the latecomer and the Karaite challenge, of the בעל שם טוב's circle, and of the Vilna Gaon's.

The week's takeaway: the seemingly minor texts of ליל שבת — אנא בכח, כגוונא, במה מדליקין — are not filler. Each is a deliberate insertion by a specific master at a specific moment, designed to shape the worshiper's transition from חול into שבת in a particular way. To say them with awareness of their history is to participate, however briefly, in the conversations that produced our siddur.

מראה מקומות

  1. שם בן מ״ב — the forty-two letter Name. — The שם בן מ״ב, derived from the opening verses of בראשית (per one tradition) or built from the seven words of אנא בכח (per the kabbalistic tradition), is referenced in היכלות רבתי and developed in early kabbalistic literature including ספר הבהיר and ספר יצירה commentaries. The structural correspondence — seven verses of אנא בכח, each opening with one word of the seven-word שם — is the key reason the prayer is built the way it is. ↩
  2. ר' נחוניא בן הקנה, traditional author. — ר' נחוניא בן הקנה is the תנא from whose בית מדרש much of the early kabbalistic-mystical tradition emerges (cf. ברכות כ״ח ע״ב; היכלות רבתי; the attribution of ספר הבהיר in some kabbalistic traditions). The traditional attribution of אנא בכח to him reflects this association rather than a halachic citation. Academic scholarship dates the prayer's first attested liturgical use to early kabbalistic circles in 12th–13th century Provence/Spain. ↩
    The traditional attribution to ר' נחוניא בן הקנה is widely accepted in Jewish liturgical tradition but is not directly attested in early Talmudic or Geonic sources. The earliest manuscript witnesses to אנא בכח as a unit appear in early kabbalistic siddurim of the 13th century.
  3. קידושין ע״א ע״א — the שם בן מ״ב; חגיגה י״א ב. — Sefaria (Kiddushin 71a:13): the forty-two-letter Name "may be transmitted only to one who is discreet, and humble," and the passage goes on to the further conditions (past middle age, not quick to anger or drunkenness, not insistent on his rights). That is the Talmudic source for the existence of the שם. The statement that the world was created through it is not in this passage; the shiur linked it instead to the משנה in חגיגה י״א ב (Sefaria: "nor may one expound the act of Creation … before two or more") and to the תוספות there, which the Rav reported but which were not located in the Sefaria text. ↩
  4. ערוך השולחן, אורח חיים סימן רס״ב; שבת קי״ט ע״א. — The ערוך השולחן (Sefaria, Orach Chaim 262) says: ובחרוז האחרון עומדים וחוזרים פנינו כלפי הפתח — at the last stanza we stand and turn toward the entrance — and cites the gemara's בואו ונצא לקראת שבת המלכה. In the gemara (Sefaria, Shabbat 119a:2) Rabbi Chanina wraps himself and says "Come and we will go out to greet Shabbat the queen," and Rabbi Yannai says "Enter, O bride. Enter, O bride." Corrected: the earlier citation of the שולחן ערוך סימן רס״ב with a רמ״א was not supported (Sefaria: Shulchan Arukh OC 262:1 concerns setting the table). The custom of standing for the entire לכה דודי was described in the shiur as that of חב״ד and, tentatively, Syrian communities. ↩
  5. טור, אורח חיים סימן ר״ע (Ashkenazic omission of במה מדליקין on יום טוב שחל בערב שבת). — Sefaria (Tur OC 270, Hebrew): ונוהגין באשכנז שלא לאומרו בי״ט שחל להיות בערב שבת מפני שאין יכולין לומר עשרתם. This concerns במה מדליקין only. Corrected: the earlier citation of שולחן ערוך או״ח סימן תס״ח is wrong (Sefaria: Shulchan Arukh OC 668 treats Shemini Atzeret), and the משנה ברורה citation was not verified. The omission of לכה דודי on such a night is described in the shiur as a matter of community custom; no source for it was located. ↩
  6. משנה שבת פרק ב (במה מדליקין); שבת ב:ז. — The second perek of מסכת שבת deals with the laws of שבת candles: which oils and wicks may be used. The closing mishnah (Sefaria, Mishnah Shabbat 2:7): "There are three things a person must say in his home on Shabbat eve at nightfall and not before"; the shiur identified them as tithing, eruv and kindling the lights (עישרתם, עירבתם, הדליקו את הנר). The perek is approximately 5–7 minutes when recited at conversational pace. ↩
  7. רב נחשון גאון; סדר רב עמרם גאון — the latecomers. — The shiur attributed the reason of the latecomers (shuls outside the city, the perek recited so that the latecomer could finish and walk home with the others) to רב נחשון גאון, and the custom of saying the perek after קדיש at the end of davening to רב עמרם גאון and רב נחשון גאון. Corrected: the earlier attribution to the טור in the name of the Geonim was not supported; the Tur (Sefaria, OC 270) gives only the reason that the perek contains the law of kindling and the three things a person must say at home. The Geonic texts themselves were not retrieved. ↩
  8. מחזור ויטרי and other Rishonim; אבודרהם, סדר ליל שבת; בית יוסף או״ח ר״ע. — The shiur gave the reminder reason in the name of the מחזור ויטרי and other Rishonim, and the proposal to say the perek before מעריב in the name of the אבודרהם (ר' דוד אבודרהם, 14th century, Sevilla). Sefaria (Beit Yosef OC 270, Hebrew): ויותר נכון לאומרו קודם כמנהג בני ספרד שע״י קריאת פרק זה יזכור הג׳ דברים שצ״ל בתוך ביתו; after מעריב מה תועלת יש לו שיזכור הרי אין בידו לתקן מעתה. The מחזור ויטרי and the אבודרהם themselves were not retrieved. ↩
  9. The Karaite reason (שמות ל״ה:ג׳). — The Rav gave this reason in the name of "some Rishonim" without naming the source. Corrected: the earlier attribution to the בית יוסף was not supported (the retrieved text of Beit Yosef OC 270 does not mention the Karaites). The verse is Sefaria, Exodus 35:3: "You shall kindle no fire throughout your settlements on the sabbath day." ↩
    The Rishonic source for the Karaite reason was not identified.
  10. ענן בן דוד (8th century, Babylon). — ענן בן דוד is the figure traditionally identified as the founder of the Karaite sect, although the modern academic literature distinguishes ענן and his immediate followers (sometimes called Ananites) from the broader Karaite movement that consolidated in the 9th century. The general narrative — that ענן, after being passed over for the position of ראש גלות, broke from the rabbinic establishment and rejected the תורה שבעל פה — is the standard account; the shiur gave the details (grandson of רב חנינאי גאון, who died in 770; a Karaite synagogue in Ashdod named for him; a Karaite synagogue in the Old City of Jerusalem; his grave on Har HaZeisim) on the Rav's authority, and none of these was checked. ↩
  11. זוהר, פרשת תרומה (דף קל״ה ע״א) — כגוונא דאינון מתייחדין. — The passage in the זוהר is the one printed in the siddur before מעריב on Friday night; it speaks of the removal of the harsh judgments (כל שולטני רוגזין) on שבת. The daf reference was not verified: the Sefaria English Zohar, Terumah, retrieved opened with the Terumah verse and the passage was not located. The earlier characterization of its contents and of its concluding phrase was not supported by the recording and has been removed. ↩
  12. בעל שם טוב הקדוש — takanah for כגוונא in the Friday-night davening. — The integration of the כגוונא passage into the Friday night davening between קבלת שבת and מעריב is attributed to the בעל שם טוב (ר' ישראל בן אליעזר, 1698–1760) and his תלמידים. The shiur stated that there is no source for the practice before the בעל שם טוב and his תלמידים; this was not checked, and scholars may differ. ↩
    The exact takanah of the בעל שם טוב is preserved through Chassidic oral and printed tradition rather than through a single signed document. The first printed Chassidic siddurim (late 18th century) consistently include כגוונא in this position.
  13. הפיאסעצנער רבי — ר' קלונימוס קלמיש שפירא (1889–1943); אש קודש; חובת התלמידים. — ר' קלונימוס קלמיש שפירא, a descendant of נועם אלימלך of Lizhensk, was the rebbe of Piaseczno before WWII and one of the most influential mashpiyim of the pre-war Chassidic world. He was murdered in the Holocaust in 1943; his manuscripts, buried in milk cans in the Warsaw Ghetto, were recovered after the war. אש קודש contains his Shabbos drashas in the ghetto. חובת התלמידים, the work from which the Rav cited the passage on כגוונא, is a guide for students. ↩
    The exact location of the passage on כגוונא within חובת התלמידים was not given in the shiur and has not been verified.
  14. חיי אדם מאת ר' אברהם דאנציג (1748–1820); סדר התפילה בנוסחו. — ר' אברהם דאנציג, a תלמיד of the Vilna Gaon and author of the popular halachic compendium חיי אדם, was a businessman who supported himself, and, per the Rav, a close follower of the Vilna Gaon (the Rav added that the two were related by marriage; this was not checked). The Rav said the חיי אדם is the first source for placing במה מדליקין between קבלת שבת and ברכו; not located: Sefaria's Chayei Adam 31 is on the Torah reading and does not mention it, and the actual passage was not found. The polemical motive is the Rav's theory. ↩
